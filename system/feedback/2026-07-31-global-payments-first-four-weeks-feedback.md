## Overall Perspective

My first four weeks have reinforced the opportunity I saw when joining Global Payments. The people have been the clear highlight. I have found the team welcoming, knowledgeable, and willing to help, and Ryan's support has been especially valuable. He has made himself available, provided context, and helped me begin navigating a large and complex organization.

I am also encouraged by the opportunity to connect Genius more deliberately with the broader Global Payments and Worldpay organization. There is meaningful potential here, although the first few weeks have also shown me where stronger onboarding, clearer operating processes, and simpler systems could help new employees become productive faster.

## Where the Experience Could Improve

### 1. Systems and access

The systems environment has been the most difficult part of onboarding. There are numerous systems, credentials, access rules, and security restrictions to navigate. Limitations such as restricted copy and paste add friction to otherwise simple work. OneDrive has also been inconsistent when searching for or locating files.

The practical impact is that I still spend a meaningful amount of time determining:

- which system contains the information I need;
- whether I have the correct access;
- which credentials or authentication method apply;
- where the current version of a file is stored; and
- how to move information between approved tools.

**Suggested improvement:** Give new hires a role-specific systems map that identifies each required application, its purpose, the access owner, login method, important restrictions, and the first three tasks the employee should be able to complete in it.

### 2. Information architecture

Useful information exists, but it can be difficult to find or determine whether it is current. File naming, storage locations, and OneDrive search do not always make the authoritative source obvious.

**Suggested improvement:** Establish a clearly owned starting point for sales resources, product materials, pricing, account information, and operating procedures. Each major resource should have an owner and a visible last-updated date.

### 3. Quoting process

The hardware quoting process appears highly manual. At this stage, it is not clear that it has the process definition, controls, or visibility needed for consistent execution at scale.

My first quote for IKEA illustrated the challenge. The process relies on a manual spreadsheet in which the available products and product distinctions are not always clear. I also did not find a standard, clearly documented process for determining customer pricing. This makes it difficult for a new team member to know whether the right products, current costs, pricing logic, and approvals are being applied consistently.

Areas that would benefit from clarification include:

- who owns each step;
- what information is required before a quote begins;
- which pricing and configuration sources are authoritative;
- how reviews and approvals are handled;
- where quote status is tracked;
- how revisions are controlled; and
- what turnaround time the sales team and customer should expect.

**Suggested improvement:** Document a standard hardware-quoting workflow with an intake checklist, named owners, approval rules, version control, status visibility, and expected service levels. Longer term, evaluate where configuration and pricing can be automated.

### 4. Team structure and account visibility

The team structure feels relatively loose, and there does not appear to be a consistently visible view of who is working on which accounts, what activity is underway, or where help is needed. This creates a risk of duplicated work, missed coordination, and inconsistent customer engagement.

**Suggested improvement:** Create a shared account-coverage view showing the account owner, supporting roles, active opportunities, recent activity, next action, and any cross-functional dependencies. A lightweight, consistently maintained view would be more useful than a complex system that is not current.

### 5. Onboarding sequence

The available training was helpful, but the overall onboarding experience could be more deliberately sequenced. Product education, systems access, sales processes, account coverage, and introductions sometimes feel like separate activities rather than one path toward role readiness.

**Suggested improvement:** Organize the first 30 days around clear milestones:

1. understand the organization and team;
2. secure and validate all required system access;
3. complete foundational product training;
4. learn the core sales and quoting workflows;
5. review account ownership and active pursuits; and
6. demonstrate readiness through a realistic customer or quoting exercise.

Although a manager cannot directly solve all of the underlying systems limitations, the onboarding experience can make those limitations easier to navigate. A standardized, role-specific onboarding plan would give new teammates a clearer sequence, identify the right resources and contacts, and reduce the amount of time spent discovering how the organization works.

- Provide Worldpay RMs with concise Genius 100-level education: what the platform does, the problems it solves, relevant customer pain, and useful discovery questions.

- Use feedback from new employees to identify recurring onboarding friction and remove it systematically.

- For each account, document the Worldpay relationship owner, known customer relationships, current technology and payments environment, opportunity hypothesis, likely Genius product fit, information gaps, and next action.

## Closing Perspective

I remain enthusiastic about joining Global Payments and about the opportunity in front of us. The people, the breadth of capability, and the potential to connect Genius with the larger payments organization are significant strengths.

My principal observation after four weeks is that the organization has strong people and substantial capability, but employees must navigate considerable systems and process complexity to put those strengths to work. Simplifying that experience—or at least making it easier to navigate—would help new hires contribute faster and allow the team to spend more time on customers and growth.

I am happy to be part of the team and am looking forward to having an impact. I am sharing these observations in that spirit and would be glad to help improve any of these areas as I continue learning the organization.
