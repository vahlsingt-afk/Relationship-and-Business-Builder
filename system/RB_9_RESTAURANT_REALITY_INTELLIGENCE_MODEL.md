# RB 9 Restaurant Reality Intelligence Model

**Date:** 2026-05-27
**Status:** authoritative design principle
**Scope:** daily brief, market intelligence, restaurant technology evaluation, Chief of Staff recommendations

## Foundational Principle

RB prioritizes decision quality over worldview reinforcement.

The system must test active theses against reality. It should surface supporting evidence, contradictory evidence, countertrends, strategic blind spots, and confidence changes. A thesis is a living model, not a belief to protect.

## Daily Brief Posture

The daily brief is the newspaper at the door: verified, prepared, delta-first, and action-ready before the user asks.

A canonical brief must show:

- verified source telemetry;
- overnight deltas;
- autonomous discovery proof or explicit discovery gap;
- macroeconomic pressure stacking;
- thesis challenges and contradictory evidence;
- restaurant pain mapping;
- franchisor/franchisee alignment gaps;
- action lifecycle state;
- delivery verification;
- canonical release status.

## Restaurant Reality Model

Restaurant technology intelligence must evaluate every signal through six lenses:

1. **Technical capability:** can it work?
2. **Operational survivability:** can it survive Friday night?
3. **Economic reality:** can restaurants afford it at scale?
4. **Organizational friction:** can the brand deploy and support it?
5. **Trust recovery burden:** has the industry been burned before?
6. **Educational burden:** how much belief change is needed before adoption?

Technical excellence is not enough. A solution can be technically strong and still fail because it is too expensive, politically fragile, operationally brittle, or trust-damaged.

## Pain Mapping

RB classifies restaurant industry pain into:

- known solvable pain;
- operationally unsolved pain;
- economically constrained pain;
- trust-damaged pain;
- politically constrained pain;
- culturally normalized pain;
- theoretically solved but practically broken pain.

Critical pattern: some pain has become normalized because operators no longer believe it can be solved. These normalized operational scars matter because they create both hidden opportunity and adoption resistance.

Examples:

- inventory is never accurate;
- managers always work 70 hours;
- integrations always break;
- rollout support always disappoints;
- SaaS tools always add another fee.

## Educational Burden

Many restaurant technologies require belief transformation before product evaluation.

The actual buying journey is often:

1. awareness of hidden cost;
2. belief the problem is solvable;
3. belief the solution survives operations;
4. belief deployment will not worsen operations;
5. belief ROI exceeds disruption;
6. belief support will exist after rollout;
7. product evaluation.

Most vendors skip to step 7. RB should flag that gap.

## Unit Economics

Operators buy against restaurant P&L reality:

- EBITDA pressure;
- labor percentage;
- food cost;
- cash flow;
- implementation cost;
- support burden;
- subscription fatigue;
- actual measurable ROI.

Many operators increasingly experience SaaS as greens fees for staying in business, not as innovation investment. RB should test pricing realism and ROI credibility accordingly.

## Franchisor / Franchisee Alignment Gap

Franchisors often optimize for top-line sales, standardization, reporting visibility, customer data, brand consistency, and enterprise control.

Franchisees optimize for bottom-line profitability, labor savings, simplicity, support responsiveness, survivability, and clear cash impact.

The **alignment gap** is the distance between franchisor value perception and franchisee value perception. The larger the gap, the more politically fragile the rollout.

Each brand is a political ecosystem. RB should track franchisee influence, governance style, deployment history, co-op power, vendor trust, technology fatigue, rollout scars, approval paths, and standardization tolerance.

## Operational Truth Detection

Reality usually appears in:

- support tickets;
- rollout failures;
- workarounds;
- abandoned deployments;
- operator resistance;
- margin compression;
- integration failures;
- staffing workarounds;
- manual overrides.

RB should optimize for operational truth detection over polished vendor narrative.

## Action Lifecycle Intelligence

RB tracks state transitions, not isolated recommendations.

For every recommendation, RB should know whether the action is:

- recommended;
- drafted;
- sent/completed;
- awaiting response;
- inside the normal waiting window;
- overdue;
- stale;
- suppressed because already completed;
- ready for reassessment.

If a follow-up was sent yesterday, RB should suppress redundant outreach and recommend the reassessment window instead.
