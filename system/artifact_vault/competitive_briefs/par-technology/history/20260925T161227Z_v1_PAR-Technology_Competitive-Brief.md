# PAR Technology — Competitor Intelligence Profile

*Last evidence: 2026-09-12 | Last synced: 2026-09-25T16:12:10Z | 22 evidence record(s)*

## Competes with Genius on
back_office, digital_ordering, loyalty_engagement, payments, pos, restaurant_os_platform

## Positioning
PAR has assembled real products (Brink/PAR POS, Data Central, Punchh, digital ordering) but they don't work seamlessly together and lack a bundled pricing story -- "portfolio, not platform." Real tech debt in the POS product, NPS not strong; benefits from POS stickiness (switching cost) rather than satisfaction. Markets itself as "open" (integrate anything), which helps land new deals but works against a unified cross-sell narrative. Reports footprint at the product level (60.1K Operator Cloud sites + 121.8K Engagement Cloud sites, ~March 2026), not unique-location level -- makes total footprint look larger than true platform penetration.

## Todd's POV
Todd Vahlsing’s interpretation of Wendy’s selecting TRAY: despite PAR’s incumbent loyalty relationship with Wendy’s, PAR was unable to cross-sell its other products into this decision. This is Todd’s strategic interpretation, not a confirmed statement from Wendy’s or PAR.

## Gap analysis vs. Genius
- **Genius wins:** Tighter system control via Xenial+RTI+Sycom lineage -- more vertically integrated than PAR's assembled/open approach
- **Genius wins:** RTI heritage gives deeper back-office/franchise-reporting maturity than PAR's Data Central
- **Genius wins:** Payments+platform bundling is structural: Global Payments already has payments, PAR is still assembling this
- **PAR Technology wins:** PAR is perceived as more modern/cloud-native in demos; cleaner sales narrative
- **PAR Technology wins:** PAR's "open" integration strategy is easier to plug into a brand's existing heterogeneous vendor stack

## Strengths
- (2026-09-11) PAR Ordering was reported to be winning roughly 50% of its opportunities, producing approximately five to six logo wins per quarter, with about half displacing a large legacy ordering provider. Every reported Ordering deal included payments, with card-not-present transactions highlighted as attractive economics. — *Todd-supplied PAR update summarizing Savneet Singh comments at Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, 2026-09-10; underlying transcript referenced but not attached.*
- (2026-09-11) PAR reportedly acquired Bridg for approximately $20 million. Singh said Bridg identity resolution could potentially double ARR for some loyalty customers and that PAR's largest restaurant loyalty customer had already signed for Bridg. This positions Guest360 as an extension of Punchh from known loyalty members toward a broader identifiable guest population. Reliability and Punchh churn remain open watch items. — *Todd-supplied PAR update summarizing Savneet Singh comments at Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, 2026-09-10; underlying transcript referenced but not attached.*

## Weaknesses
- (2026-09-11) PAR's strategy increasingly depends on multi-product adoption. The supplied update reports that 100% of new Q2 deals included more than one PAR product, versus roughly 70–80% previously. This strengthens PAR when the suite is accepted but creates vulnerability if one or more components are not competitive; Punchh churn remains an unresolved watch item. — *Todd-supplied PAR update summarizing Savneet Singh comments at Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, 2026-09-10; underlying transcript referenced but not attached.*

## Reference customers
- (2026-09-12) Todd Vahlsing states that PAR is Wendy’s loyalty provider. — *Todd Vahlsing, 2026-09-12*

## Customer wins
- (2026-09-11) PAR's Burger King rollout was reportedly running above 400 stores per month, ahead of schedule, and expected to be substantially complete by year-end 2026. Singh described the volume as unprecedented for PAR and said the post-rollout opportunity is cross-selling back-office products into the Burger King estate, with relatively little incremental growth expected from additional Burger King POS locations. — *Todd-supplied PAR update summarizing Savneet Singh comments at Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, 2026-09-10; underlying transcript referenced but not attached.*
- (2026-09-11) Singh reportedly described Papa Johns as PAR's first major pizza implementation and acknowledged PAR had to build pizza-specific capabilities involving modifiers, oven management, delivery, and third-party ordering. PAR subsequently won Pizza Factory and another smaller pizza chain, indicating the Papa Johns work is becoming a reusable pizza-vertical product. — *Todd-supplied PAR update summarizing Savneet Singh comments at Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, 2026-09-10; underlying transcript referenced but not attached.*

## Positioning notes
- (2026-09-11) Savneet Singh reportedly named Global Payments, NCR Voyix, and Oracle as legacy competitors that retain restaurant-tech market share but do not primarily focus on restaurant technology, contrasting them with PAR's restaurant specialization. He also said RFP activity increased over the prior 12 months and that sales engineering is the one PAR department adding headcount because of RFP and demo volume. — *Todd-supplied PAR update summarizing Savneet Singh comments at Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, 2026-09-10; underlying transcript referenced but not attached.*
- (2026-09-11) Singh reportedly described PAR Intelligence as Assist → Advise → Action. Assist provides conversational access to restaurant data and is free; PAR said it was live at about 20,000 sites and targeted roughly 50,000 Assist locations. Advise adds predictive recommendations. Action is intended to execute operational actions and begin monetization in 2027. PAR's example was automatically pausing third-party delivery ordering when drive-thru times become excessive. Singh's longer-term vision is for PAR Intelligence to become the primary interface across PAR POS, back-office, and other applications. Activated locations demonstrate distribution, not independently verified operational value. — *Todd-supplied PAR update summarizing Savneet Singh comments at Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, 2026-09-10; underlying transcript referenced but not attached.*
- (2026-09-11) Singh reportedly said PAR sees no indication that large restaurant companies want to return to building their own POS systems, arguing that omnichannel complexity and franchise economics make internally developed POS increasingly unattractive. PAR's strategic framing is therefore platform consolidation rather than build-versus-buy. — *Todd-supplied PAR update summarizing Savneet Singh comments at Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, 2026-09-10; underlying transcript referenced but not attached.*
