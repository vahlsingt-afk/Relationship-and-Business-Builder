# Restaurant-Tech and Operator Watchlist Definition

**Purpose:** define the company universe RB should monitor for restaurant-tech and restaurant-operator industry signals in daily briefs, relationship signals, opportunity detection, and network-gap work.

This is not a generic tech-news universe. RB should monitor two connected markets:

1. **Restaurant-tech vendors:** the top 30-50 companies that materially shape restaurant operations technology, especially companies likely to show, sponsor, speak, or launch around NRA and similar restaurant/hospitality events.
2. **Restaurant brands/operators:** the top public, scaled private, franchise, QSR, fast-casual, casual dining, convenience/foodservice, and emerging multi-unit brands whose technology priorities reveal buyer demand, operational pain, and AI/automation adoption.
3. **Strategic operator entities:** large franchise operators, multi-brand franchise groups, PE-backed restaurant operators, operator holding companies, consolidators, high-influence franchisee groups, and regional scale operators whose movements change restaurant-technology demand, influence peer operators, or create future relationship opportunities.

## Inclusion Rule

Include a company when it sells, enables, embeds, finances, integrates, or materially influences technology used by restaurant operators, franchise systems, QSR/FSR brands, hospitality groups, or restaurant-tech vendors.

Primary categories:

- **POS platforms:** cloud POS, enterprise POS, SMB POS, franchise POS, POS middleware.
- **Back office:** inventory, labor, scheduling, accounting, procurement, food cost, analytics, above-store tools.
- **Payments:** acquiring, integrated payments, pay-at-table, processor-owned restaurant platforms, fraud/risk, stored value.
- **Loyalty and CRM:** guest data, loyalty, offers, marketing automation, CDP, customer engagement.
- **Digital menu boards and drive-thru display:** menu-board software/hardware, content management, order-confirmation displays.
- **Online ordering and delivery enablement:** first-party ordering, marketplace integrations, delivery orchestration, catering, order throttling.
- **Restaurant AI:** voice AI, upsell AI, order accuracy, forecasting, labor optimization, support automation, guest personalization.
- **POS hardware:** terminals, handhelds, kiosks, kitchen display systems, printers, scanners, payment devices.
- **Drive-thru camera and communication devices:** headsets, timers, cameras, vehicle detection, lane orchestration, computer vision.
- **Kitchen and ops execution:** KDS, production timing, make-line tools, food safety, IoT, equipment monitoring.
- **Guest experience / CX:** reservations, waitlist, review management, guest messaging, table management.
- **Enterprise restaurant integration layer:** APIs, middleware, data platforms, reporting, multi-unit system orchestration.

## Restaurant Brand / Operator Demand Watch

Monitor top restaurant brands because they reveal the demand side of restaurant technology: where budgets are going, which pain points matter, and which technologies are moving from pilot to strategic priority.

Include brands/operators when they are:

- Public restaurant companies with earnings calls, investor decks, annual reports, or strategic priorities that mention technology, digital, AI, labor, drive-thru, loyalty, delivery, or operational efficiency.
- Large private or franchise systems with visible technology initiatives, supplier partnerships, conference participation, press releases, or executive interviews.
- Emerging multi-unit brands that are scaling quickly and adopting technology as an operating model advantage.
- Brands connected to Todd's network, active opportunities, consulting themes, target accounts, or market positioning.

Buyer-side categories to monitor:

- **AI as strategic pillar:** AI in ordering, forecasting, labor, marketing, guest personalization, support, kitchen ops, or above-store analytics.
- **Digital sales mix:** app/web ordering, delivery, first-party ordering, marketplaces, catering, subscriptions.
- **Loyalty and guest data:** membership growth, personalization, offer strategy, CRM/CDP investment.
- **Drive-thru and throughput:** speed of service, lane design, computer vision, headsets, timers, order accuracy, menu-board optimization.
- **Labor and productivity:** scheduling, labor forecasting, automation, manager tools, training, retention.
- **Back-office modernization:** inventory, food cost, procurement, accounting, reporting, enterprise data.
- **POS and platform transitions:** POS replacement, platform consolidation, vendor rationalization, integration pain.
- **Franchisee adoption:** technology rollout friction, franchisee economics, compliance, support, change management.
- **Store operations pain points:** accuracy, speed, consistency, waste, equipment uptime, training, customer experience.

Operator signal sources:

- Earnings call transcripts, 10-K/10-Q filings, investor presentations, annual reports, and strategic priority decks.
- Brand press releases, executive interviews, conference sessions, and franchisee communications when public.
- LinkedIn/social posts from brand executives, franchisees, operators, CIOs/CTOs, digital leaders, and known network contacts.
- Vertical trade coverage focused on brand technology priorities, not generic restaurant consumer news.
- Vendor case studies only when they name the operator problem, rollout scope, measurable outcome, or executive owner.

## Strategic Operator Intelligence

This is not a news enhancement. It is a strategic intelligence and relationship-prioritization layer for the CoS engine.

RB should monitor operator entities such as Flynn Group, Carrols Restaurant Group, Sailormen, Ghai Management, Thrive Restaurant Group, Sizzling Platter, KBP Foods, MuY Companies, NPC successor entities, and comparable regional or PE-backed operators.

Signals to track:

- acquisitions, divestitures, bankruptcies, restructures, franchise transfers, and PE investment activity.
- concept expansion, geographic expansion, unit growth velocity, and multi-brand behavior.
- executive hires/departures, operations leadership movement, and technology leadership movement.
- technology standardization signals, vendor transitions, implementation hiring, reporting/analytics needs, and cross-brand operating-model changes.
- labor pressure, integration strain, operational complexity, and store-execution pressure.

For each significant movement, RB must infer and label:

- operational complexity increase
- standardization pressure
- likely technology reassessment
- scaling strain
- integration risk
- labor coordination pressure
- reporting visibility needs
- vendor consolidation likelihood
- future buying-window probability

The output must map relationship proximity, network overlap, mutual connections, existing vendor relationships where known, strategic importance, ecosystem influence, operational pressure, and follow-up priority. RB should promote durable entities into persistent watchlists: Strategic Operators, Multi-Brand Operators, Expansion Leaders, High-Influence Franchisees, and PE-Backed Groups.

## Source Priority

RB should monitor this universe through:

- LinkedIn/social posts from company leaders, operators, buyers, analysts, and known network contacts.
- Vertical trade and hospitality sources.
- Public-company primary data: earnings call transcripts, investor presentations, SEC filings, market data, stock-price moves, press releases, product announcements, job postings, partner announcements, and company blogs.
- Event signals: NRA exhibitors/sponsors/speakers, conference sessions, product demos, launch announcements, booth themes, partner ecosystems.

Mainstream business news is allowed only as corroboration, financing/M&A context, or broad macro framing. It should not be the primary source for restaurant-tech monitoring.

## Monitoring Standard

For each monitored company signal, RB should classify:

- `company`
- `category`
- `side` (`vendor_supply` or `operator_demand`)
- `source_type`
- `source_quality`
- `event_at`
- `signal_type`
- `macro_force` when the item is macro/geopolitical rather than a company event
- `pain_point_or_priority`
- `strategic_relevance`
- `affected_relationships_or_threads`
- `restaurant_operator_impact`
- `restaurant_tech_vendor_implication`
- `second_order_impact`
- `relationship_opportunity`
- `why_this_matters_to_todd`
- `recommended_action`
- `timing_priority` (`today`, `this_week`, or `monitor`)
- `confidence`
- `strategic_operator_entity_type` when the entity is an operator group, franchisee group, PE-backed operator, consolidator, or holding company
- `strategic_operator_movement` when the event is an acquisition, divestiture, bankruptcy, restructure, expansion, leadership move, franchise transfer, vendor transition, or technology standardization signal
- `influence_score`
- `relationship_value_score`
- `operational_pressure_score`
- `ecosystem_impact_score`
- `future_opportunity_score`
- `relationship_proximity`
- `known_network_overlap`
- `mutual_connections`
- `existing_vendor_relationships`
- `buying_window_probability`
- `follow_up_priority`
- `watchlist_bucket`

The brief should never stop at "headline + source." Each meaningful item
must answer: what changed, why it matters, how it affects restaurants, how it
affects restaurant tech, who in Todd's network matters now, what Todd should
do next, and how urgent it is.

For strategic operator movements, the brief should additionally answer: who is becoming strategically important, why they matter operationally, how they influence the ecosystem, where relationship investment should occur, and what future opportunities may emerge.

The default action can be `ignore` when the signal is interesting but does not affect Todd's relationships, opportunities, positioning, or active threads.

## Watchlist Size

Target 30-50 active restaurant-tech vendors plus a parallel watch of top restaurant brands/operators. The list should evolve, but each company should fit at least one category above and have a plausible reason RB would care:

- Todd has a relationship path into the company.
- The company is in an active opportunity or job-search thread.
- The vendor affects restaurant-operator buying behavior.
- The brand reveals buyer demand, technology priorities, pain points, or AI/automation adoption.
- The company is a direct or adjacent competitor/partner to known target companies.
- The company is creating a market signal Todd can use for positioning, consulting, or content.

## Out of Scope

- Generic SaaS companies with no restaurant/hospitality operating relevance.
- Consumer food brands unless the signal concerns their technology stack, operating pain, AI/digital priorities, store execution, or buying behavior.
- General AI news unless tied to restaurant operations, hospitality workflows, or a monitored company.
- Mainstream business headlines with no relationship, operator, or vendor implication.
