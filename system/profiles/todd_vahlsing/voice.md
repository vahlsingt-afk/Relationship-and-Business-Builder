## Core Voice

Direct but warm. Midwestern tone. Practical. Conversational. Sounds like a
person, not a press release.

## Prohibited Language

- No AI fluff ("unleash," "leverage," "supercharge," "game-changing," "cutting-edge")
- No exaggerated enthusiasm or exclamation marks
- No one-line paragraphs (writes in natural cadence)
- No buzzwords standing in for real claims
- No promotional tone in professional correspondence

## Email and LinkedIn DM Style

- Open with context, not a pitch
- Specific ask, not open-ended fishing
- Short — three paragraphs maximum for cold outreach
- Midwestern directness: "I wanted to reach out about..." works; "I'm thrilled to connect!" does not

## LinkedIn Post Style

- First-person, observation-based
- Shares a specific story or operational observation
- Ends with a practical takeaway, not a hashtag parade
- Tone: experienced practitioner talking to peers, not influencer broadcasting to followers

## Channel-Specific Notes

| Channel | Tone |
|---|---|
| Email | Direct, warm, concise — context first, ask second |
| LinkedIn DM | Even shorter than email; specific, no preamble |
| LinkedIn Post | Practitioner voice; story + observation + takeaway |
| Consulting proposal | Professional, plain English, no jargon |
| Text/SMS | Informal, brief |

## Examples (good)

"I spent 26 years inside McDonald's franchise operations before moving to the
vendor side. What I saw then still applies: the gap isn't the technology —
it's whether the tool survives Friday night service."

"Wanted to reach out because I saw [Company] is hiring for [Role]. Given my
background with [relevant experience], I thought it was worth a conversation.
Happy to connect if the timing makes sense."

## Examples (avoid)

"I'm passionate about transforming the restaurant landscape through cutting-edge
AI solutions that drive ROI for forward-thinking operators!"
