# Executive Summary: Worldpay/Genius Transaction-Metered POS Initiative

**Prepared for:** Ryan Hildebrand  
**Prepared by:** Todd Vahlsing  
**Date:** August 10, 2026  
**Status:** Internal concept for feasibility and sponsorship review; not approved for customer presentation

## Decision Requested

Should Worldpay and Genius launch a structured feasibility effort for a new commercial initiative that prices Genius POS—and potentially other Genius software and services—as a fixed cost per eligible transaction, subject to a defined monthly floor and ceiling, instead of billing a traditional recurring SaaS subscription?

Before discussing this concept with Five Guys or any other customer, we need answers to two questions:

- **Can we physically and operationally do this?**
- **Does the business have the will to offer something this disruptive?**

The immediate request is not approval to make a Five Guys offer. It is approval to investigate a repeatable Worldpay/Genius commercial model and assemble the appropriate commercial, finance, product, billing, legal, risk, implementation, and support leaders to determine whether it is viable. Five Guys would serve as the first candidate design and pilot account, subject to feasibility, sponsorship, account validation, and customer fit.

This is **transaction-metered software pricing**, not a processing subsidy. The payment transaction is the usage meter and may also become the collection rail. The Genius software-and-services fee remains a separately defined, fixed dollar amount per eligible transaction; it is never a percentage of payment volume and should be reported separately and transparently from card-processing charges.

The working monthly formula is:

> **Monthly Genius software-and-services fee = the lesser of the ceiling or the greater of the floor and eligible transaction count × fixed software-and-services fee per transaction.**

## Initiative Structure

This should be developed as a Worldpay/Genius initiative—not as a one-account pricing exception.

- **Program thesis:** Give qualified Worldpay customers a transaction-metered way to purchase Genius software and services while protecting processing relationships and improving the combined Worldpay/Genius economic model.
- **First candidate design account:** Use Five Guys' transaction profile, incumbent economics, global requirements, franchise structure, and renewal calendar to test the model.
- **First candidate pilot:** If internal feasibility and customer fit are established, seek approval for a tightly bounded Five Guys pilot or initial deployment.
- **Repeatability gate:** Use the design work to determine eligibility rules, standard economics, systems requirements, approval authority, and which other Worldpay customers could qualify.

This framing preserves the larger strategic value without allowing the creation of a broad program to delay the first real-world test.

## The Concept

Create a Worldpay-customer-exclusive transaction-metered software-and-services option with these working characteristics:

- No separate traditional Genius SaaS invoice awaiting customer payment.
- A separately stated fixed software-and-services fee assessed per eligible Worldpay card transaction—not a percentage of sales and not a blended processing-rate uplift—with fees beginning at the first eligible production transaction.
- A clearly defined monthly floor and ceiling, likely at the restaurant level. The floor protects the cost of delivering and supporting the service; the ceiling gives high-volume locations predictable maximum exposure.
- Three- or five-year price protection.
- An optional accelerated-activation incentive: customers that meet an agreed deployment or go-live milestone can earn a defined number of service-fee credit months. The underlying transaction-metered service begins at activation; the incentive is an earned credit, not an open-ended pricing promise.
- Device-agnostic deployment: customers may reuse viable Windows hardware and transition to iOS or Android devices during normal replacement cycles.
- API-first interoperability that preserves the customer's ability to retain preferred digital, back-office, loyalty, delivery, and other technology providers.
- One Genius software foundation for U.S. and international operations, subject to market-level validation of payments, language, hardware, certification, implementation, and support requirements.

It is a usage-based software-pricing model in which POS expense moves with transaction activity within a transparent minimum and maximum range. A time-bound activation credit can reward faster deployment without changing the long-term economic model.

## Why Five Guys Is a Strong First Candidate

Five Guys provides a timely and strategically relevant test case:

- Worldpay already has the payment relationship and above-store transaction volume we want to protect.
- Todd's recollection is that the PAR contract ends or renews in March 2027; Avery independently described the renewal as spring 2027. The exact expiration and notice dates still require confirmation.
- Current PAR POS pricing is believed to be approximately $140 per restaurant per month, with an increase expected during renewal. This is field intelligence and must be validated before external use.
- Five Guys' CTO, M.J., values vendor autonomy and is reluctant to concentrate multiple products with one provider. A modular, API-first offer could respect that preference rather than force consolidation.
- Five Guys has international operations and has reportedly encountered NCR Aloha availability limitations in some countries. A consistent global software platform could eliminate the need to maintain separate domestic and international POS strategies.
- Genius has relevant scale proof through CKE/Hardee's and Dutch Bros selections. The exact contracted, deployed, transaction-volume, support, and referenceable evidence must be assembled before making customer claims.

Five Guys should be treated as the first candidate design and pilot account, not as an approved customer offer. No concept should be presented to M.J. until Worldpay/Genius leadership confirms initiative sponsorship, feasibility, economics, pilot authority, and customer-facing boundaries.

## Customer Value Proposition

### Protect cash flow

POS expense follows eligible card activity rather than arriving only as a fixed SaaS charge. Customers begin funding the service when production transactions begin. The ceiling protects high-volume restaurants, while transaction-based pricing gives lower-volume periods relief subject to the agreed service floor.

For cash-constrained owner-operators and franchisees with subscription fatigue, paying for software a few cents at a time as revenue is generated is materially different from receiving another $150 monthly technology bill. It aligns expense to activity, reduces bill shock and a separate payment decision, and makes the cost easier to absorb within daily restaurant cash flow.

> **Pay for the technology as the restaurant earns—not as another bill waiting at the end of the month.**

### Create price certainty

A fixed per-transaction fee, defined floor and ceiling, and three- or five-year price lock provide an alternative to recurring SaaS increases.

### Extend existing hardware investment

Customers can reuse serviceable Windows devices and migrate to iOS or Android at the natural replacement point instead of funding an immediate chainwide hardware event.

### Preserve technology choice

The model should not require customers to replace favored providers. Genius should compete as an open POS foundation that works with the customer's chosen restaurant-technology ecosystem. The commercial eligibility requirement would be maintaining Worldpay processing for in-store and above-store transactions, subject to internal feasibility and approval.

### Operate on one global software foundation

Customers can pursue a consistent POS platform across domestic and international markets while using locally appropriate hardware, payments, integrations, and support models.

## Strategic Value to Worldpay and Genius

- Protect and deepen valuable Worldpay processing relationships.
- Provide Worldpay relationship managers with a differentiated retention and expansion instrument.
- Create a reason for customers to evaluate Genius before an incumbent POS renewal becomes automatic.
- Shift the competitive comparison from one SaaS subscription versus another to transaction-metered software pricing.
- Increase Genius distribution through Worldpay's installed customer base without requiring a generic full-stack consolidation pitch.
- Improve the combined Worldpay/Genius value story while preserving transparent service and customer choice.
- Establish a repeatable offer for selected Worldpay restaurant customers if the Five Guys economics and operating model prove viable.
- Begin earning service revenue with the first eligible production transaction rather than waiting until the end of a monthly invoice cycle, subject to accounting and settlement validation.
- Reduce accounts-receivable exposure if the fee can be calculated and collected through existing Worldpay settlement or billing rails.
- Reduce invoice generation, payment-delay, collections, reconciliation, and bad-debt work associated with a separate SaaS receivable.
- Use earned activation credits to align the customer and delivery teams around faster go-live dates and earlier transaction volume.

## Feasibility Questions: Can We Do It?

### Economics and accounting

- What fixed per-transaction fee, monthly floor, and monthly ceiling produce acceptable unit economics?
- Are the floor and ceiling applied per location, legal entity, franchisee, or enterprise?
- How should the floor work for low-volume, seasonal, temporarily closed, newly activated, or partially deployed locations?
- How would revenue be recognized, allocated, forecast, and reported between Worldpay and Genius?
- Can fee accrual and collection begin with the first eligible production transaction, and what is the cash and revenue-recognition timing?
- What activation milestone should earn service-fee credits, how many months may be credited, and how are those economics approved?
- Can the model include implementation, integrations, support, upgrades, and hardware services without creating unacceptable margin exposure?
- How do the economics compare with the current Genius SaaS model and Worldpay retention value?

### Billing and technical execution

- Can current Worldpay and Genius systems identify eligible transactions and calculate the fee and cap accurately?
- Can current systems enforce both the monthly floor and ceiling accurately?
- Can the separately stated POS software fee be collected transparently through settlement or existing billing rails without generating a separate receivable or being confused with or blended into payment-processing rates?
- Can the system produce a customer-facing statement showing eligible transactions, accrued POS fees, floor or ceiling adjustments, credits, and amounts collected?
- How would refunds, voids, chargebacks, offline transactions, card-not-present orders, non-card tenders, and cross-border transactions be treated?
- Can corporate and franchise locations be billed and reported correctly across multiple merchant IDs and legal entities?
- What happens if a location changes processor, pauses operations, transfers ownership, or leaves the brand?

### Legal, compliance, and contracting

- Can eligibility be conditioned on maintaining a Worldpay processing relationship, and under what legal and commercial constraints?
- How must the offer distinguish a fixed technology fee from a percentage-based payment charge?
- Are there franchise, merchant-pricing, tax, disclosure, or international regulatory implications?
- What termination rights, transition assistance, data portability, and post-term pricing are required?
- Can we give customers a credible exit path without undermining the processing-retention objective?

### Delivery and global readiness

- Which Five Guys integrations and operational requirements are supported today?
- Can existing Windows hardware be certified and supported, and what minimum specifications apply?
- Which iOS and Android devices are supported for future replacement?
- In which Five Guys markets can Genius software, implementation, payments, language, hardware, and support be delivered today?
- What named enterprise support and governance model would be required?
- What evidence from CKE/Hardee's and Dutch Bros can be used to demonstrate scale and reduce adoption risk?

## Strategic Questions: Do We Have the Will?

- Is leadership willing to challenge the conventional per-location SaaS model to protect and grow strategic Worldpay relationships?
- Are Worldpay and Genius willing to evaluate value across the combined processing and software relationship rather than optimize each product's margin independently?
- Would this be a tightly controlled strategic-account exception, a repeatable Worldpay customer program, or a formal new pricing model?
- Who has authority to sponsor and approve the experiment?
- What customer and account criteria would prevent the offer from becoming an uncontrolled discount mechanism?
- Are we prepared to support a three- or five-year price commitment?
- Can sales compensation and account ownership be aligned so Worldpay and Genius teams benefit from the combined outcome?
- If the model works, are we willing to use it proactively against incumbent POS systems across the Worldpay portfolio?

## Proposed Internal Path

1. **Ryan sponsorship:** Decide whether the concept merits a rapid internal feasibility review.
2. **Decision team:** Name owners from Worldpay/Genius finance, commercial strategy, billing, product, legal, risk, implementation, support, and international operations.
3. **Five Guys fact base:** Confirm the PAR expiration, notice deadline, pricing, store/merchant structure, processing scope, global estate, integration requirements, and decision process.
4. **Economic model:** Test multiple fixed-fee, floor, ceiling, and accelerated-activation-credit structures against Five Guys transaction distributions and combined Worldpay/Genius economics.
5. **Operational validation:** Determine whether existing billing and settlement capabilities can administer the model or whether a manual/limited pilot structure is required.
6. **Executive gate:** Decide whether to stop, approve a Five Guys pilot path, or proceed with both the pilot and a repeatable Worldpay/Genius program.
7. **Customer approach:** Only after approval, coordinate with Avery on how and when to introduce the concept to M.J.

## Recommended Guardrails

- No customer discussion before written internal sponsorship and feasibility validation.
- No percentage-of-sales POS charge.
- A visible, contractually defined floor and ceiling with transparent customer reporting.
- Any service-fee credit must be earned against a specific activation milestone, time-bound, and explicitly approved.
- No forced adoption of unrelated products or displacement of preferred providers.
- No unsupported global-coverage or enterprise-scale claims.
- Clear eligibility criteria, unit economics, approval authority, and exception controls.
- A defined customer exit and transition mechanism.

## Bottom Line

The opportunity is larger than a Five Guys motion. Worldpay and Genius may be able to create a new commercial category: transaction-metered POS software with a defined floor and ceiling for qualified processing customers. Five Guys provides the first concrete design case and potential pilot, while the initiative creates the strategic rationale for investing in reusable billing, contracting, enablement, and operating capabilities. It could protect payment relationships, create a differentiated Genius entry point, improve customer cash flow, reduce receivables and collections work, accelerate activation, preserve technology choice, and support one global software foundation.

The next move is internal. We need Ryan's approval to investigate the Worldpay/Genius initiative, using Five Guys as the first candidate design and pilot account. The team must determine whether the model is physically possible, whether the business has the strategic will to pursue it, and whether Five Guys is the right first application. Until those gates are cleared, this remains an internal hypothesis and should not be presented to M.J.
