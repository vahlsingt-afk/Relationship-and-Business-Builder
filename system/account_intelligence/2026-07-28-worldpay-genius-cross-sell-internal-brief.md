# Worldpay + Genius Enterprise Account Development Strategy

**Internal discussion draft | Revised 3 August 2026**  
**Strategy owner:** Todd Vahlsing

## Executive Summary

Worldpay has trusted, long-standing relationships with enterprise restaurant and hospitality customers. Genius brings restaurant operating expertise and technology capabilities across POS, payments, back office, digital menu boards, drive-thru, hardware, and guest engagement.

The opportunity is to bring those strengths together through a coordinated, long-term account-development strategy.

This is not a passive referral program and it is not a broad product-pitch campaign. We will use Worldpay relationship equity to earn warm introductions, build relationships beyond finance and payments, understand each customer's technology priorities, and develop account-specific points of view about the value of connecting payments with the broader restaurant technology stack.

Pain signals and active buying events will accelerate opportunities, but we will not wait for them before building the relationships and account understanding required to compete.

## What We Are Building

We are creating a repeatable motion that:

1. equips Worldpay relationship managers with practical Genius 100-level knowledge;
2. uses RM-sponsored introductions to widen customer relationships;
3. builds a joint account plan for each selected customer;
4. earns Genius participation in customer QBRs, SBRs, and roadmap discussions;
5. develops a relevant payments-plus-technology storyline;
6. converts that relationship and insight into qualified opportunities over time.

## The Operating Model

| Stage | What happens | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Select** | Todd and the RM identify a priority account with strategic value, relationship access, or a credible long-term technology storyline | Selected account and working hypothesis |
| **Understand** | Confirm the existing Global Payments/Genius engagement, customer stack, lifecycle, internal ownership, and prior activity | Verified current state |
| **Introduce** | The RM sponsors introductions to the appropriate customer stakeholders | Warm customer access |
| **Widen** | Build relationships across technology, operations, marketing, franchise leadership, and executive sponsorship | Broader relationship map |
| **Plan** | Todd and the RM maintain a joint Blue Sheet account plan | Storyline, stakeholder map, and actions |
| **Engage** | Participate in QBRs, SBRs, roadmap sessions, industry events, and customer discovery | Customer insight and next commitments |
| **Advance** | Pursue a specific product motion when customer fit, interest, and timing align | Qualified opportunity |
| **Refine** | Re-score the account and adjust the storyline, stakeholder plan, product posture, and resource level as new facts emerge | Current priority, revised hypothesis, and next-best action |

The model is intentionally iterative. Account rank, relationship coverage, competitive posture, and timing will change as the team learns. “Refine” is therefore a required operating step—not an occasional cleanup exercise.

## How the RM Cadence Works

### Every-other-week account sessions

Todd and each RM will use the existing cadence to:

- select and review priority accounts;
- confirm current Global Payments activity;
- identify the relationships that need to be widened;
- plan warm introductions;
- develop the account storyline;

- assign one next action, owner, and date.

The Blue Sheet should be scaled to the account and the RM. Start with a one-page minimum viable version for every selected account, then deepen it only when the relationship, opportunity, or risk justifies the work. The objective is better decisions and customer access—not completion of a document.

Before launch, confirm with Brian how RMs are compensated and measured, including account retention, payment-volume protection, expansion, introductions, and cross-sell participation. Position the cadence in the language of the outcomes each RM is responsible for. Where incentives differ, tailor the participation model rather than assuming one uniform approach.

### Every-other-week team enablement

Todd will join the RM team meeting for short Genius 100-level sessions.

Each 10–15 minute module will explain:

- what the capability does;
- what restaurant problem it solves;
- what pain or change signals to recognize;
- what questions the RM can ask;
- when and how to bring Todd into the conversation.

Initial modules will cover the Genius platform, POS, payments integration, back office, digital menu boards, drive-thru/hardware, and loyalty/guest engagement.

The training should distinguish the full Genius platform from a POS-only pitch. It should also help RMs identify threats to Worldpay payment volume from vertically integrated platforms such as Olo Payments, Qu Pay, and PAR Payments, while recognizing wedge products that can create value even when POS displacement is not credible.

Use David Lee as an early sounding board for the enablement sequence because he understands the transition from a Worldpay/payments lens into the broader restaurant-technology platform. Invite selected RMs to relevant Genius sales training when the content is useful at a 100-level and does not require deep product expertise.

## The Blue Sheet

The Blue Sheet is the joint Worldpay–Genius account plan. It is created proactively; an active RFP or known pain point is not required.

Each plan includes:

- verified Global Payments/Genius activity and ownership;
- current restaurant technology and payments stack;
- platform selection, deployment, and renewal timing;
- customer priorities and hypotheses to test;
- account-specific payments-plus-technology storyline;
- target product or adjacency, when credible;
- stakeholder and relationship map;
- upcoming customer touchpoints;
- next actions, owners, and dates.

The portfolio workbook remains the source of truth for prioritization, RM ownership, technology evidence, and account scoring. The Blue Sheet provides the deeper relationship and execution plan.

Blue Sheets should live in one shared repository with visible ownership and revision dates. A completed sheet is not an achievement by itself. Each review must result in a customer commitment, a verified fact, a relationship advance, a clear disposition, or an explicitly scheduled next action.

## Building Relationship Width

Worldpay relationships frequently begin in finance or payments. Enterprise technology decisions require broader coverage.

| Buying center | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Finance and payments** | Payment economics, gateway strategy, reporting, fraud, and total cost |
| **Technology** | POS, architecture, integration, security, hardware, and implementation |
| **Operations** | Back office, labor, inventory, throughput, support, and rollout execution |
| **Marketing and digital** | Digital menu boards, loyalty, guest data, promotions, and digital ordering |
| **Franchise leadership** | Adoption, economics, deployment, local support, and operator value |
| **Executive sponsorship** | Strategic alignment, organizational priority, and decision support |

Every active account plan should identify the existing Worldpay relationship, the missing buying-center relationships, and the next warm introduction required.

## Events and Customer-Access Motion

Events are an underused bridge between the Worldpay and Genius teams and should be managed as part of account development rather than as a separate marketing activity.

For each priority account, record:

- the customer's event calendar and expected attendees;
- which Worldpay and Genius events matter to the account;
- whether the RM attends customer events, sends another representative, or does not participate;
- the specific relationship or discovery objective for each event;
- the warm-introduction owner and target stakeholder;
- the required follow-up and Blue Sheet update.

For FS/TEC, identify one or two RMs whose customers are attending, provide passes where useful, and pre-book RM-sponsored meetings with technology, operations, marketing, or digital leaders. Booth traffic is not the objective; warm access and a defined next commitment are.

Use the same discipline for QBRs, SBRs, monthly reviews, customer conferences, and restaurant-adjacent Worldpay events. Attendance should always have a stated account objective.

## Product Strategy

We will match the product motion to the customer's current technology lifecycle.

- If a POS decision remains open, POS and integrated payments may be appropriate.
- If a POS was recently selected, we will not reopen that decision. We will evaluate adjacencies such as digital menu boards, hardware, drive-thru, loyalty, back office, or payments optimization.
- Existing Genius customers will be treated as adoption and expansion opportunities—not new logos.
- Difficult deployments may create a problem-solving or adjacency opening, but we will not assume the customer wants to replace the platform.
- Unknown technology layers will be researched rather than treated as automatic whitespace.

Leading indicators:

- RMs trained and modules delivered;
- proactive warm introductions;
- Blue Sheets created and updated;
- QBR/SBR participation;
- new technology, operations, marketing, and franchise relationships;
- buying-center coverage;
- accounts with an articulated payments-plus-technology storyline.

Quality and adoption indicators:

- percentage of selected accounts with an RM-agreed next action;
- percentage with verified internal ownership and no unresolved collision;
- introductions that reach a target buying center, not merely a generic contact;
- Blue Sheets updated because of new evidence or customer movement;
- RM participation and usefulness feedback by cadence;
- event and QBR meetings that produce a verified fact, relationship advance, or next commitment.

Commercial outcomes:

- customer discovery meetings;
- qualified opportunities;
- Genius expansion;
- Worldpay payment volume protected or expanded;
- combined Worldpay–Genius wins.

## Guiding Principles

- Be proactive, thoughtful, and patient.
- Build relationship width before expecting a transaction.
- Lead with a relevant customer point of view, not a product catalog.
- Connect payments to the broader technology and operating environment.
- Respect recent customer platform decisions.
- Treat pain and buying events as accelerators, not prerequisites.
- Preserve RM trust and close the loop after every customer interaction.
- Do not mistake activity for achievement; every artifact and meeting must advance a decision, relationship, verified fact, or customer commitment.
- Refine account rank and strategy continuously as evidence changes.
