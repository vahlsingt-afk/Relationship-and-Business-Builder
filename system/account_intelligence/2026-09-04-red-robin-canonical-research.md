## Account snapshot

- Red Robin Gourmet Burgers, Inc. (NASDAQ: RRGB) is a publicly traded casual-dining brand founded in 1969 and headquartered in Englewood, Colorado.
- At fiscal year-end December 28, 2025, the system had 475 restaurants: 385 company-owned and 90 franchised across the United States and one Canadian province. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $1.210 billion, down 3.1% year over year; comparable restaurant revenue declined 0.3%, excluding the 0.4% unfavorable impact of deferred loyalty revenue.
- Fiscal 2025 restaurant-level operating profit margin improved to 12.7% from 10.8% in fiscal 2024. The company closed 22 company-owned restaurants and one franchised restaurant during the year.
- In Q2 2026, revenue was $277.6 million, down from $283.7 million, while comparable restaurant revenue rose 1.3%. Guest traffic declined 0.2%, average check rose 1.5%, and restaurant-level operating profit margin improved 20 basis points to 14.7%, the strongest second-quarter margin since 2022. Adjusted EBITDA was $18.9 million versus $22.4 million in the prior-year quarter.
- As of July 12, 2026, Red Robin reported $167.2 million of borrowings and approximately $47.8 million of liquidity. Fiscal 2026 guidance, issued before the completed refranchising transactions were incorporated, called for comparable restaurant revenue growth of 0.5%–1.5%, restaurant-level operating profit of approximately 13.0%, adjusted EBITDA of $70–$73 million, and capital expenditures of $25–$30 million.

## Strategic context

Red Robin is executing its **First Choice Plan**, launched in July 2025 under President and CEO Dave Pace. The plan is designed to make the brand the first choice for guests, team members, and investors while preserving progress from the prior North Star Plan. Current operating themes include restoring traffic, sharpening value through the Big Yummm platform, improving labor and operating efficiency, empowering Managing Partners, modernizing restaurants and technology, and strengthening the balance sheet.

The most consequential 2026 change is a major refranchising program. On September 1, Red Robin said it had completed the sale of 108 company-owned restaurants for approximately $89.4 million and expected the remaining eight units to close by fiscal year-end for another $6.6 million. The full program covers 116 restaurants and approximately $96 million of proceeds. The new operators are Op Burgers, Kuber, and Evergreen Dining. Red Robin intends to use proceeds to reduce debt, refinance its capital structure, and continue investing in restaurants and technology.

This creates a materially different account environment. Technology decisions must now account for a larger franchise-operated share, three significant new operators, possible differences between corporate and franchise deployment economics, and the need for common standards with practical operator-level implementation. The refranchising event is a credible discovery trigger; it is not evidence that a POS or payments sourcing event is active.

## Technology environment

### Confirmed or source-backed

- **Payments — Worldpay:** RB carries Worldpay as confirmed at high confidence. Avery Churchwell is the identified Worldpay relationship manager for Red Robin in the Worldpay Master Account Plan. Exact processing scope, gateway, channels, devices, contract term, renewal dates, and franchise coverage remain unverified.
- **Point of sale — NCR Aloha:** The Worldpay Master Account Plan identifies NCR Aloha as the mature incumbent estate. This is internal account intelligence and should be validated directly with Avery and Red Robin before external positioning.
- **Pay-at-table — Ziosk:** Ziosk's current public website displays Red Robin among its restaurant-brand customers. The Worldpay plan identifies dissatisfaction and a possible replacement angle, but the nature, severity, store coverage, and decision status of that dissatisfaction are not customer-confirmed.
- **Digital ordering — Olo:** The Worldpay plan identifies Olo in the stack. Red Robin publicly offers online ordering, delivery, catering, curbside, and pickup, but current Olo modules and store/channel coverage require validation.
- **Catering — MonkeyMedia:** The Worldpay plan identifies MonkeyMedia in the stack. Product scope, ownership, and current deployment should be confirmed.
- **Loyalty and app — Red Robin Royalty:** The company relaunched Royalty in Q2 2024. Members generally earn points for dollars spent and redeem rewards at stated thresholds. The mobile app supports ordering, customization, rewards, curbside, pickup, and delivery. The underlying loyalty technology vendor is not established by the public sources reviewed.
- **Restaurant systems:** The 2025 Form 10-K says Red Robin uses food-waste and labor-management systems, sales and forecasting tools, inventory management, operational-execution technology, centralized finance/accounting/HR systems, and an operations dashboard integrated with centralized data. The filing does not name the vendors.
- **Infrastructure and kitchen:** Red Robin said it completed infrastructure-modernization work in fiscal 2025 to improve technology performance, stability, and security, including kitchen-display-system upgrades and other restaurant IT. It spent $13.7 million on technology, infrastructure, and related capital in fiscal 2025, up from $9.8 million in fiscal 2024.

### Important qualification signals

- The 2025 10-K says Red Robin has replaced large portions of technology hardware in recent years, while some systems remain aging. The company explicitly identifies upgrade complexity, vendor reliability, outages, cybersecurity, and implementation risk as material concerns.
- The September 1 refranchising announcement says the company expects to continue reinvesting in restaurants and technologies. That supports technology discovery, but capital discipline and debt reduction remain central constraints.
- The company-versus-franchise governance model must be mapped. Red Robin sets standards and systems for franchisees, but ownership, funding, deployment, and payments decisions may vary across corporate restaurants, legacy franchisees, and the three new large operators.

However, total revenue and adjusted EBITDA were lower year over year, guest traffic remained slightly negative, debt was still material before transaction proceeds, and management remains focused on capital discipline. The correct conclusion is “improving but still constrained,” not “fully recovered.”

## Opportunity hypotheses — not yet qualified facts

1. **Pay-at-table modernization:** If Ziosk dissatisfaction is real and material, Genius could explore a replacement or complementary table-service workflow tied to guest convenience, faster payment, labor efficiency, table turns, and reduced device/support complexity.
2. **Payments economics and estate rationalization:** Worldpay access may support a fact-based review of acquiring, gateway, device, and channel economics. The value case must account for corporate and franchise coverage, existing contracts, and any NCR-linked dependencies.
3. **Refranchising transition support:** The addition of three large operators creates a need to clarify technology standards, deployment ownership, support boundaries, reporting, security, and commercial consistency across the changing estate.
4. **Hardware lifecycle:** The disclosed mix of recent hardware replacement and remaining aging systems creates a discovery path around store cohorts, support deadlines, device health, replacement capital, and deployment sequencing.
5. **Digital and loyalty integration:** A new CMO and long-tenured technology leader may create a useful business-and-technology alignment conversation around Royalty, digital ordering, payments, guest identity, and data—without assuming a platform replacement.

## Risks and cautions

- Do not treat improved Q2 operating results as proof of a technology budget or active sourcing event.
- Do not assert that Red Robin is replacing Ziosk until dissatisfaction, sponsor, scope, and decision status are confirmed directly.
- Do not assume the Worldpay relationship covers every channel, company store, franchisee, or new refranchise operator.
- Do not position a broad POS displacement before validating NCR contract term, system performance, integration dependencies, and available capital.
- Refranchising increases potential reach but also fragments the buying process. Map corporate standards and operator autonomy before proposing enterprise scope.
- Distinguish Chief Transformation Officer John McLaughlin from Chief Technology Officer Deena DePhilips in every stakeholder map.

7. Are Op Burgers, Kuber, and Evergreen Dining required to use corporate platforms and processors, or do they have decision rights?

9. What outcomes matter most now: traffic, labor productivity, table turns, payment cost, guest satisfaction, loyalty frequency, uptime, or franchisee economics?
10. Is there an approved 2026–2027 technology investment roadmap, and where could a narrowly scoped pilot fit without competing with debt reduction and restaurant reinvestment?
