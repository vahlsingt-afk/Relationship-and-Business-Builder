# Chief of Staff Brief — Ryan Hildebrand Feedback on Worldpay Development Plan

**Meeting:** 3 August 2026, 8:59 a.m.  
**Source:** Just Press Record capture `cap-3654fabbc0e36a68`  
**Participants:** Todd Vahlsing and Ryan Hildebrand

## Bottom Line

Ryan strongly endorsed the strategy, the account-ranking discipline, the Blue Sheet approach, and Todd's early work. His feedback was not a change in direction; it was a request to make the plan easier for Worldpay RMs to adopt, more explicitly tied to their incentives, more deliberate about event-based customer access, and more iterative as account facts change.

The most important management signal is that Ryan is enthusiastic about Todd's leadership in this work. He repeatedly offered support, said he was impressed with the strategy, and was especially interested in seeing the training and Blue Sheets take shape. The risk is execution drag: a sound strategy could become administrative activity unless every Blue Sheet, training session, and meeting produces a relationship advance, verified fact, customer commitment, or explicit next action.

## Ryan's Explicit Feedback

1. **Keep the Blue Sheet model.** Ryan likes the structure and believes it will improve account strategy and RM development.
2. **Design for adoption.** Blue Sheets are strenuous; RM capability, available time, and willingness will vary.
3. **Tie the work to RM economics.** Ask Brian how RMs are compensated and measured so the strategy is framed around retention, protection, expansion, or other outcomes that matter to them.
4. **Use events as an access channel.** The two teams currently operate separately around events. Customer events, FS/TEC, QBRs, and business reviews can accelerate warm introductions and direct customer relationships.
5. **Add “Refine” to the operating model.** Account ranking, stakeholder maps, vendor position, timing, and product posture will change. Refinement should be a formal eighth stage.
6. **Preserve account-specific history.** Internal knowledge is fragmented and often lives in people's heads. Current ownership and prior activity must be reconciled before outreach.
7. **Clarify key account motions.** GoTo Foods needs an ownership decision; Subway has a proprietary POS and a global Genius KDS deployment; Slim Chickens requires careful stakeholder navigation; RDS engagements must follow the dealer/channel model.
8. **Build a payments capability map.** Gateway, processor, fraud, dispute, gift, and other add-on availability varies. Both teams need a shared view of what can be sold together.
9. **Continue the enablement launch.** Ryan supported the 20-minute training plus Q&A format and suggested the material should keep the full platform—not only POS—in view.
