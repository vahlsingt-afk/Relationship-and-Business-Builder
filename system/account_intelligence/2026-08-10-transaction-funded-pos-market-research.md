# Market Research: Transaction-Metered POS Without a Separate SaaS Invoice

**Date:** August 10, 2026  
**Purpose:** Determine whether the proposed Worldpay/Genius transaction-funded POS initiative is disruptive in the enterprise restaurant market or already offered by payment/POS competitors.  
**Research posture:** Public-source assessment using official vendor pricing, product pages, investor materials, and SEC disclosures. Enterprise contracts are commonly confidential; absence of a public offer is not proof that no bespoke agreement exists.

## Executive Conclusion

The broad idea of eliminating a visible monthly POS software charge by recovering economics through payments is **not new**. Toast, SpotOn, Square, Shift4, and Global Payments/Genius all publicly use some form of payments-linked subsidy, zero-dollar entry plan, or bundled hardware/software economics. Adyen and Stripe also normalize transaction-priced, no-monthly-fee payment infrastructure at enterprise scale, although they do not supply a complete restaurant POS application.

The proposed Worldpay/Genius model is still **potentially disruptive at the enterprise restaurant level** because it is more precisely a transaction-metered software model—not a processing subsidy—and no reviewed public source documents the full combination:

- enterprise-grade restaurant POS;
- a fixed POS technology fee per eligible card transaction rather than a percentage of sales;
- a contractually defined floor and ceiling;
- collection through payment or settlement rails with no separate SaaS invoice awaiting payment;
- three- or five-year price protection;
- reuse of viable Windows hardware and gradual migration to iOS or Android;
- open/API-first preservation of other technology providers; and
- one POS software foundation across U.S. and international operations.

The defensible claim is therefore not “the first POS without SaaS fees.” The stronger claim is:

> **Enterprise POS software priced as a fixed cost per eligible transaction, with transparent minimum and maximum economics, collected alongside payment activity while preserving hardware and ecosystem choice.**

The distinction matters: payment volume does not subsidize or hide the POS cost inside a processing percentage. Each eligible transaction meters a separately defined fixed software charge until the ceiling is reached. The floor protects the minimum economics of providing the service.

Do not claim “industry first” until confidential competitive contracting and legal diligence are complete.

## What Is and Is Not Disruptive

### Not disruptive by itself

- Saying “$0 monthly POS” or “no SaaS fee.”
- Subsidizing hardware or software through a higher processing rate.
- Requiring the vendor's payment processing to receive preferred POS economics.
- Charging per transaction for payment infrastructure.
- Using go-live bonuses or waived/credited months to accelerate activation.
- Offering a processing minimum or multi-year agreement.

These mechanisms are already visible in Toast, SpotOn, Square, Shift4, and Global Payments/Genius offers.

### Potentially disruptive in combination

- Applying the model to a complex global enterprise restaurant rather than a single-location or small-chain merchant.
- Separating the **POS technology charge** from card-processing economics as a fixed amount per eligible transaction, not a percentage or blended processing uplift.
- Publishing a predictable floor and ceiling so both customer and provider can model downside and upside.
- Collecting alongside payment activity, reducing the separate SaaS receivable and collections process.
- Allowing the enterprise to reuse current Windows hardware and shift operating systems only during natural replacement.
- Preserving other best-of-breed vendors instead of using the pricing model to force platform consolidation.
- Providing one global POS software foundation across domestic and international markets.
- Locking the model for three or five years through an incumbent renewal cycle.

The disruption is the **enterprise commercial architecture and operating freedom**, not the disappearance of a line labeled “SaaS.” The clearest category name is **transaction-metered POS software**.

There is also a meaningful owner-operator experience even when total annual cost is similar. A separately stated software fee collected a few cents at a time alongside revenue can reduce bill shock, subscription fatigue, payment friction, and working-capital pressure compared with a discrete $150 monthly invoice. This is not merely presentation if collection timing changes and the operator no longer has to reserve cash, approve a bill, or wait for an ACH withdrawal after the revenue period. The value should be measured through cash timing and operator experience, not only nominal annual cost.

## Important Messaging Correction

“No more SaaS fees” is rhetorically powerful but technically vulnerable.

- The customer is still paying for software; the unit of charge changes from a subscription invoice to eligible transactions.
- A monthly floor can look economically similar to a minimum subscription charge.
- A month-end floor true-up may create a receivable unless Worldpay can net it from settlement or maintain a funded reserve/ACH authority.
- Accounting may still classify some or all revenue as subscription, stand-ready, or variable-consideration software revenue.
- Enterprise procurement will ask for the effective cost per location, not only the billing label.

Recommended language:

> **No separate SaaS invoice. POS software is priced as a transparent fixed cost per eligible transaction, within a contractually guaranteed monthly range.**

This is accurate while the accounting, billing, and legal structure is being tested.

## Competitive Implications for Five Guys

### Why the concept could gain attention

- It creates a visible alternative to accepting a PAR SaaS increase.
- It uses the existing Worldpay relationship rather than introducing a new processor solely to subsidize POS.
- It aligns expense with restaurant activity while establishing a maximum.
- It can protect low-volume franchisees without penalizing high-volume stores.
- It avoids an immediate chainwide hardware replacement.
- It supports M.J.'s preference for vendor autonomy.
- It can address Five Guys' reported international POS fragmentation.

### Likely competitive responses

- **PAR:** price protection, renewal credits, bundled PAR Pay economics, hardware concessions, or a platform-wide discount.
- **Shift4:** low monthly POS fee, subsidized hardware, signing bonus, and processing-funded package.
- **Toast:** custom enterprise processing/software bundle and transition incentives, even though the public pay-as-you-go offer is single-location.
- **SpotOn:** custom enterprise bundle based on its existing processing-funded and capped software constructs.
- **Oracle/NCR:** enterprise/global pricing concessions, hardware lifecycle support, and incumbent migration-risk arguments.

The model will not remain unique merely because it is announced. Defensibility must come from Worldpay's installed relationship, Genius product capability, global deployment readiness, contract certainty, support, and speed of execution.

## Recommended Internal Position

### What to tell Ryan

1. The market has validated payments-funded POS at smaller scales.
2. Global Payments already has a limited Genius precedent, reducing conceptual risk.
3. The white space is an enterprise-ready, transparent, fixed-per-transaction model with floor/ceiling economics and no separate SaaS receivable.
4. Five Guys is a strong design case because it combines a known incumbent-renewal trigger, Worldpay processing value, global complexity, and demand for vendor independence.
5. We should test whether existing Genius/Worldpay systems and contracts can extend the current small-merchant mechanics to enterprise without destroying margin or creating reconciliation complexity.

### Questions the research raises

- Can the current Genius no-subscription program's billing, settlement, underwriting, and accounting components be reused?
- Can a distinct fixed POS technology fee be netted from settlement without being represented as a processing markup?
- Can a store-level floor and ceiling be administered across Five Guys' merchant IDs, franchisees, currencies, and countries?
- Does the floor require a monthly true-up, and can that true-up avoid becoming conventional AR?
- What transaction types count: card-present, card-not-present, above-store, refunds, voids, and offline activity?
- Can Worldpay preserve transparent interchange/processor economics while itemizing the POS component?
- Would processing exclusivity undermine the “preserve choice” narrative, and how should it be described?
- Which markets can actually support the full Genius software, fiscalization, integration, payment, and service model?
- How would sales compensation and revenue ownership be divided between Worldpay and Genius?

## Assessment

| Are floor/cap software economics new? | No; SpotOn publicly uses a volume-based software formula with a base amount and cap. | High |
