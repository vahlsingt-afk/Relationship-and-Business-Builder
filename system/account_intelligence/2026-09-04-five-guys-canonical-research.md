## Executive summary

Five Guys is a privately held, family-controlled fast-casual restaurant system founded in Arlington, Virginia, in 1986. The brand says it is approaching 2,000 restaurants in 30 countries, employs more than 50,000 people across the system, and is still expanding globally. Its current North American franchise territories are sold out; selected international markets remain open. Five Guys also says more than 700 stores are planned over eight years, creating a long runway for deployment, support, and cross-border technology requirements.

The account is strategically attractive, but customer-directed outreach is now on explicit hold. On September 4, MJ Worsham told Global Payments that Five Guys is already working with Worldpay as a long-term customer, has no interest in evaluating other Global Payments solutions at this time, and will work through Avery Churchwell and Melissa Douglas if that changes. Worldpay's September 4 account review ranks Five Guys second in the portfolio, P1 / Tier 1, with an 82 score, but that priority does not override the customer's direction. The correct motion is account containment and Worldpay relationship stewardship—not prospecting, meeting requests, or a product pitch.

Five Guys has a mature U.S. PAR Brink estate. Public evidence supports Brink as mission-critical POS and reporting infrastructure, Olo for digital ordering and guest-data activation, and Crunchtime for brand-wide inventory, food-cost, and labor management. Global architecture appears less uniform: Worldpay intelligence identifies NCR in EMEA and warns of international support gaps. This creates a plausible modularity and global-consistency discussion, but there is no confirmed sourcing event, RFI, RFP, budget, or replacement mandate.

The immediate objective is to stop duplicate outreach, align internally with Avery and Melissa, and support Five Guys' existing Worldpay relationship. Any future discovery with MJ Worsham or Zerrick Pearson should occur only if Avery or Melissa reopens the path. A transaction-metered technology-funding concept is being explored internally, but it is neither approved nor customer-presented and must not be positioned as an offer.

## Brand profile

- **Legal / ownership:** Five Guys Enterprises, LLC; privately held and controlled by the Murrell family. The company does not publish public-company financial filings, so revenue, system sales, and profitability should not be estimated in customer-facing materials.
- **Founded:** 1986 by Jerry and Janie Murrell in Arlington, Virginia.
- **Headquarters:** Alexandria, Virginia, according to the company's contact page.
- **Scale:** Nearly 2,000 restaurants across 30 countries; more than 50,000 people across the system. The company's current franchise page separately says more than 1,950 locations across 29 countries; the FAQ is treated as the newer footprint statement.
- **Format and proposition:** Made-to-order burgers, hot dogs, fries, and shakes; a deliberately focused menu, high customization, fresh ingredients, open kitchens, and a strong quality-and-consistency culture.
- **Franchise model:** The brand began franchising in 2003. North American territories are sold out and not currently offered; selected international markets remain open. Published requirements include $5 million minimum net worth, $2.5 million liquidity, a $25,000 franchise fee, a $50,000 development fee, and a 6% royalty.
- **Growth:** Five Guys' July 2025 real-estate criteria said more than 700 stores were set to open over the following eight years. The 2026 media fact sheet says the brand is closing in on 2,000 global restaurants in its 40th anniversary year.
- **Loyalty stance:** Five Guys' current FAQ explicitly says the brand does not offer a rewards or loyalty program. That does not mean it lacks first-party engagement: Olo documents large-scale guest profiles and personalized email marketing.

## Current business situation

Five Guys is balancing global expansion with a tightly controlled brand and operating model. The family business marked its 40th anniversary in February 2026 and cited nearly 2,000 restaurants across five continents. The brand's growth story is not merely domestic unit expansion; it increasingly requires consistent restaurant operations, local-market flexibility, cross-border support, and governance across corporate and franchise locations.

Digital guest engagement has become materially more sophisticated even without a points-based loyalty program. Olo reports that Five Guys built 8.1 million guest profiles, attributed $16 million in online revenue to guests who engaged with email campaigns, and recorded a 7.4% increase in overall sales between 2023 and 2024. Those are vendor-reported case-study results, not audited Five Guys disclosures, but they demonstrate that first-party data activation is already an executive priority.

The February 2026 40th-birthday promotion exposed the operational importance of digital reliability. Five Guys publicly acknowledged sporadic online and app-ordering issues during unusually high demand, along with stores running out of product and closing early. This should not be framed as chronic instability; it is a recent example of how digital-channel resilience, capacity, and restaurant execution converge during demand spikes.

## Technology environment

### Confirmed or source-backed

- **POS and reporting — PAR Brink:** Internal Worldpay intelligence identifies a mature U.S. PAR estate. A 2026 Novatio case study describes Five Guys' POS and Brink Reporting applications as mission-critical, with automated testing used enterprise-wide for upgrades. This is stronger evidence than generic partner references, though exact store counts, software versions, hardware, payment integrations, renewal term, and international exceptions remain unknown.
- **Back office, inventory, food cost, and labor — Crunchtime:** Crunchtime says Five Guys selected its restaurant-management system for corporate and franchise locations worldwide. Its current case materials cite more than 1,700 locations and report that food-cost variance fell below 1% within a year. The scope and performance claims are vendor-reported and should be validated with Five Guys.
- **Digital ordering and guest data — Olo:** Olo describes Five Guys as a longtime digital-ordering customer and documents use of its Guest Data Platform and Marketing products. The current case study reports 8.1 million guest profiles, behavior-based automations, and $16 million in online revenue associated with email-engaged guests.
- **Quality assurance / test automation — UiPath through Novatio:** Novatio reports an enterprise-wide automated testing framework for POS and Brink upgrades using UiPath Test Bots, with a claimed 400% improvement in testing-cycle time. Treat the quantified outcome as vendor-reported.
- **EMEA POS — NCR:** Worldpay Master Account Plan intelligence identifies NCR in EMEA. No current public primary source was found that establishes products, countries, store counts, or contractual scope; keep this as high-value internal discovery intelligence rather than a confirmed public deployment.
- **Payments — Worldpay / Global Payments:** Internal account intelligence identifies an existing Worldpay relationship but does not yet establish exact products, countries, channels, acquiring entities, processing volume, device estate, or renewal dates.
- **Payment devices — Verifone:** The Worldpay plan identifies Verifone in the estate. Device models, ownership, support, gateway/acquirer relationships, and geographic scope remain unverified.
- **Delivery / restaurant integration — LineTen:** The Worldpay plan identifies LineTen. Publicly validated Five Guys scope was not located during this research cycle, so function, deployment, and geography require confirmation.

## Opportunity hypotheses — not yet qualified facts

1. **Future global modularity and support:** A mature Brink U.S. estate plus reported NCR EMEA footprint may eventually create a case for a modular platform, shared services, or consistent support in markets where the current architecture is fragmented. This is a dormant hypothesis, not a current sales motion or global replacement thesis.
2. **Payments and device harmonization:** Worldpay's current relationship may support improved geographic coverage, reporting, device lifecycle management, or economics if the legal-entity and channel map reveals fragmentation.
3. **Selective restaurant modernization:** New openings, refreshes, nontraditional venues, franchise cohorts, or countries outside the core Brink standard may provide lower-disruption entry points.
4. **Transaction-metered technology funding:** A capped per-transaction construct could reduce upfront friction if commercially and legally approved. It must first be modeled internally and tested against merchant economics, processor scope, franchise governance, and term risk.
5. **Resilience and release assurance:** Five Guys' investment in automated testing shows that safe POS upgrades matter. Genius can explore coexistence, deployment control, monitoring, and support value without criticizing the installed platform.
6. **Data and digital continuity:** Olo demonstrates significant first-party data activation. Any Genius proposition should preserve ordering, guest identity, attribution, and reporting continuity rather than introducing a competing loyalty narrative.
