## Executive Summary

The opportunity is larger than selling a POS product into Worldpay accounts. Worldpay relationship managers already have trusted access, recurring customer conversations, and detailed knowledge of the payments environment. Genius brings restaurant operating experience and a broader platform that can solve problems around POS, payments, digital ordering, hardware, drive-thru, back office, and adjacent restaurant workflows.

The strategy is to combine those strengths:

1. Use Worldpay RMs as the account-sensing layer.
2. Screen accounts for operator pain, technology change, payment leakage, and competitive lock-in.
3. Build a concise, account-specific point of view before involving the customer.
4. Enter through the problem—not through a generic product pitch.
5. Coordinate ownership, discovery, solution design, and follow-through across Worldpay and Genius.

The initial portfolio should stay deliberately small. A handful of well-supported wins will establish the model, create internal confidence, and produce proof points that can be scaled across the broader Worldpay restaurant book.

## Strategic Objective

Build a joint cross-sell engine that:

- protects and expands Worldpay payment volume;
- creates qualified Genius pipeline;

## What We Learned From the RM Meetings

### 1. The RMs are the sensing network

RMs hear about pain during QBRs, annual reviews, implementations, support escalations, conferences, and informal customer conversations. The cross-sell motion should make it easy for them to recognize and pass along a signal without asking them to become Genius product experts.

### 2. Account trust must come before product pressure

Worldpay owns valuable long-term relationships. Genius should strengthen those relationships by bringing relevant expertise and solving a known problem. Customer introductions should happen only after the RM and Genius agree on the account hypothesis and desired outcome.

### 3. Competitive payment leakage is the central strategic threat

Toast, PAR, Qu, Oracle, Olo, and other platforms can use POS, gateway, digital ordering, data, or payfac economics to weaken Worldpay's direct relationship. An account may still ride Worldpay rails while Worldpay loses margin, control, data, or strategic relevance.

### 4. Genius needs an enterprise-ready story

Some accounts will view Genius as a credible platform; others may remember capability gaps or see it as less mature than Oracle, PAR, Qu, or Toast. We need account-specific proof, roadmap clarity, implementation confidence, and honest qualification—not a one-size-fits-all pitch.

### 5. The combined value proposition is simplification

The strongest story is not “buy another product.” It is:

> Reduce operational complexity, protect payment economics, and give the operator one coordinated team across restaurant technology and payments.

## The Cross-Sell Operating Model

### Step 1 — Sense

RMs flag observable customer signals:

- planned POS replacement, migration, or RFP;
- dissatisfaction with the current POS or implementation partner;
- fragmented store and digital payments;
- gateway cost or routing complexity;
- pressure from an embedded-payments or payfac provider;
- fraud, chargeback, or authorization-rate pain;
- online-ordering, loyalty, data, drive-thru, hardware, or back-office gaps;
- global acquiring or franchisee support complexity;
- upcoming QBR, annual review, renewal, or industry event.

### Step 2 — Screen

Todd and the RM complete a one-page account screen:

| Dimension | Required question |
|---|---|
| Account ownership | Who is the Worldpay RM and who owns the Genius motion? |
| Business priority | What operator or financial problem matters now? |
| Technology stack | POS, gateway, acquirer, devices, online ordering, loyalty/CDP, gift card, back office |
| Change event | Is there an RFP, renewal, migration, rollout, executive change, or service issue? |
| Payment exposure | Could Worldpay lose volume, economics, data, or the direct relationship? |
| Genius fit | Which specific capability could solve the problem? |
| Relationship path | Who can sponsor discovery and who decides? |
| Timing | Why now, and what happens if we wait? |
| Proof required | What evidence, reference, roadmap answer, or commercial case is needed? |
| Next action | One owner, one action, one date |

### Step 3 — Select the motion

Each screened account goes into one primary motion:

1. **Active conversion** — a live change event and credible path to a Genius/platform or payments win.
2. **Design partner / expansion** — an existing Genius or Worldpay customer that can help improve the combined enterprise offer.
3. **Payment defense** — a competitor is using POS, gateway, digital, data, or payfac leverage to reduce Worldpay's direct economics or control.
4. **Monitor / adjacency** — the relationship is valuable, but no specific customer pain or near-term opening exists.

### Step 4 — Build the account POV

Before a customer introduction, the joint team prepares:

- the customer problem in the customer's language;
- the current-state architecture;
- the cost, complexity, risk, or missed value in that state;
- the proposed future state;
- why Worldpay + Genius is differentiated;
- discovery questions and disqualifiers;
- required product, implementation, pricing, and executive support;
- the smallest useful next step.

### Step 5 — Engage and advance

The RM makes or sponsors the introduction. Todd leads restaurant-specific discovery and coordinates the right Genius/Global specialists. The team records the outcome, assigns the next action, and closes the loop with the RM.

## Dale's SDR Team as an Activation Channel

Dale's SDR team should be a formal part of the cross-sell operating model. The SDRs can create leverage by researching accounts, finding the right stakeholders, building coordinated outreach sequences, and converting events such as FS/TEC into scheduled customer conversations.

The SDR role is not to cold-pitch Genius into Worldpay's enterprise book. Their role is to activate an account hypothesis that the Worldpay RM and Todd have already aligned on.

### SDR responsibilities

- enrich the Worldpay enterprise account and contact list;
- confirm restaurant-technology, payments, finance, operations, and procurement stakeholders;
- identify known technology-change events, public priorities, and likely FS/TEC attendance;
- prepare account-specific outreach using the approved problem hypothesis;
- coordinate outreach timing and channel with the Worldpay RM;
- book discovery meetings for Todd, the RM, and the appropriate Genius specialist;
- maintain a clear disposition for every target: meeting booked, engaged, nurture, monitor, or suppress;
- return new account intelligence to Todd and the RM rather than advancing unsupported claims.

### SDR activation requirements

An account should enter SDR activation only when it has:

1. a named Worldpay RM;
2. a current problem or change hypothesis;
3. a defined target persona;
4. a relevant Genius/Global capability;
5. an agreed call to action;
6. RM approval of the outreach approach.

### SDR handoff standard

| Stage | SDR responsibility | Todd / RM responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Research | Validate contacts, current signals, and event attendance | Confirm account ownership and problem hypothesis |
| Message | Personalize the approved sequence | Approve positioning and customer context |
| Engage | Run coordinated email, phone, and LinkedIn touches | Provide warm paths and relationship context |
| Book | Schedule the right attendees with a clear purpose | Accept quickly and prepare the account POV |
| Close loop | Record outcome and next step | Update SDR and RM after the meeting |

### Messaging guardrails

- Never imply that Worldpay has disclosed confidential account information.
- Never lead with “replace your POS” unless the customer has stated that objective.
- Do not claim a product fit before discovery.
- Use the customer's known priority, not a generic list of Genius products.
- Include the Worldpay RM in outreach or explicitly reference their sponsorship.
- Suppress accounts where the RM says timing or relationship conditions are wrong.
- Return nonresponsive accounts to nurture after an agreed sequence; do not create customer fatigue.

- send the customer follow-up;
- close the loop with the RM and SDR;
- create or update the account opportunity;
- classify the outcome as advance, research, nurture, defer, or close;
- schedule the next working session before conference momentum fades.

2. Where do payments and restaurant systems create friction, cost, or risk?
3. What outcome matters over the next 6–18 months?

## Product Selection Based on POS Status

| **Migration active and decision still open** | POS is eligible if Genius can reduce risk or complexity | Genius POS + integrated payments; then required adjacencies |
| **Selected or deployed within the last 0–24 months** | Do not lead with POS replacement | Digital menu boards, drive-thru, hardware, payments optimization, implementation support |
| **Changed 24–60 months ago** | Test satisfaction and unmet roadmap needs | Adjacency first; POS only if the customer confirms material pain |
| **More than 60 months old / approaching renewal** | Full platform discovery may be appropriate | POS modernization plus payments and adjacent products |
| **Unknown** | Research before messaging | No product claim until the RM/SDR validates current POS, selection date, contract timing, and pain |
