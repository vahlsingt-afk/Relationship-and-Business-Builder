# Worldpay × Genius Cross-Sell Strategy

**Simplified working draft — 28 July 2026**  
**Owner:** Todd Vahlsing

## Strategy in One Sentence

Use Worldpay's trusted customer relationships to earn proactive warm introductions, widen relationships beyond finance, and build a long-term account storyline around the unique value of connecting payments with the restaurant technology stack.

## Objective

Create a repeatable Worldpay–Genius operating rhythm that:

- produces proactive warm introductions into priority accounts;
- brings Todd into customer QBRs and SBRs as a restaurant-technology resource;
- develops a joint Blue Sheet plan before a formal buying event appears;
- expands Worldpay's relationships beyond finance;
- builds a credible payments-plus-technology storyline for each account;
- helps RMs recognize and quickly surface problems Genius can solve;
- creates qualified Genius opportunities over time without disrupting RM trust.

This is not a passive lead-referral program. It is a thoughtful, proactive account-development campaign designed to build relationship width and strategic relevance over time.

The goal is not to turn Worldpay RMs into Genius sellers. Their role is to provide relationship context, sponsor introductions, help Todd navigate the account, and alert the team when pain or a change event appears.

## Operating Rhythm

### Every-other-week RM account sessions

Todd has completed the initial RM meetings and now has an every-other-week cadence with the RMs.

Use these sessions to:

- select priority accounts for proactive relationship development;
- identify the warm introductions needed across technology, operations, marketing, and franchise leadership;
- build and update the joint Blue Sheet;
- develop the account-specific payments-plus-technology storyline;
- plan QBR/SBR participation and other customer touchpoints;
- review new customer signals and pain when it appears;
- discuss upcoming QBRs, SBRs, renewals, technology reviews, and executive meetings;
- record one next action, one owner, and one date.

These are working sessions, not broad pipeline reviews.

### Every-other-week RM team meeting

Todd will attend the team's every-other-week meeting to build relationships and deliver short Genius 100-level training.

Each session should take 10–15 minutes and cover:

1. what the capability does;
2. the restaurant problem it solves;
3. the pain signals an RM may hear;
4. two or three questions the RM can ask;
5. when to introduce Todd;
6. one short customer example or use case.

Keep the training practical and introductory. Avoid deep product demonstrations, feature dumps, and technical architecture unless the team requests a separate session.

## Genius 100-Level Curriculum

| Module | Problem Genius helps solve | Pain to listen for | Questions for the RM |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Genius platform overview** | Fragmented restaurant technology and too many vendor/support paths | Integration problems, inconsistent reporting, unclear ownership | “Which systems create the most friction?” “Where do issues bounce between vendors?” |
| **POS** | Aging, unreliable, difficult-to-scale, or poorly supported store systems | Planned refresh, outages, slow rollout, franchise dissatisfaction | “When was the POS selected?” “Is there a renewal or refresh coming?” |
| **Payments + POS** | Disconnected payment and POS economics, routing, support, or reporting | Gateway cost, multiple acquirers, payfac pressure, fragmented store/digital payments | “Who owns the full payment flow?” “Where are economics or customer control leaking?” |
| **Back office** | Labor, inventory, food cost, forecasting, and operational visibility | Manual work, poor forecasts, inconsistent execution, margin pressure | “Which operating process is still manual?” “Where are managers losing time or margin?” |
| **Digital menu boards** | Slow, expensive, inconsistent menu and promotional changes | Manual updates, poor daypart flexibility, pricing inconsistency, hardware refresh | “How are menus changed today?” “Is there a screen or content refresh planned?” |
| **Drive-thru / hardware** | Throughput, accuracy, visibility, and aging hardware | Long service times, poor accuracy, device failures, inconsistent support | “Where does the guest journey slow down?” “What hardware is approaching end of life?” |
| **Loyalty / engagement** | Fragmented guest data, weak personalization, and poor campaign execution | Disconnected loyalty and ordering, low engagement, limited customer insight | “Who owns guest engagement?” “Can marketing connect behavior across channels?” |

## From Account Selection to Opportunity

### 1. Select the account proactively

Todd and the RM select an account because it has strategic value, relationship access, a credible technology/payments storyline, an upcoming customer touchpoint, or a long-term expansion opportunity.

Known pain is helpful, but it is not required to begin relationship development.

### 2. Build the initial account hypothesis

Confirm:

- the current restaurant technology and payments environment;
- where connecting payments with the technology stack may create distinctive value;
- the Worldpay relationships already in place;
- the missing technology, operations, marketing, and franchise relationships;
- the first useful customer conversation;
- any known pain, change event, or timing trigger.

### 3. RM makes a warm introduction

Position Todd as a restaurant-technology resource from the broader Worldpay/Global Payments team—not as a cold product seller and not only as a response to an active problem.

Suggested introduction:

> I want to introduce Todd Vahlsing, who works alongside our Worldpay enterprise restaurant customers. Todd has spent his career in restaurant operations and technology, and his role is to help customers think across payments and the broader restaurant technology stack. We would value the opportunity to learn more about your priorities, share where the combined Worldpay and Genius capabilities may be relevant, and establish Todd as an additional resource for your team.

### 4. Todd and the RM build the Blue Sheet

The Blue Sheet becomes the proactive working customer plan: relationship map, account storyline, customer-value hypothesis, and sequence of introductions and conversations.

### 5. Todd joins the customer operating rhythm

Where appropriate, Todd joins:

- QBRs and SBRs;
- technology roadmap reviews;
- renewal planning;
- executive discovery;
- FS/TEC and other industry-event meetings.

The first objective is to understand the customer's priorities, build relationship width, and establish the payments-plus-technology storyline. A specific product pursuit should still follow credible fit and customer discovery.

## Blue Sheet Account Plan

Create a Blue Sheet for each selected priority account. Do not require a known pain point or active buying event.

### Account facts

- account and Worldpay RM;
- current Global Payments/Genius engagement, opportunity owner, stage, product scope, and last activity;
- business units, brands, and franchise structure;
- current POS and selection/deployment date;
- payments, gateway, digital ordering, loyalty, back office, DMB, drive-thru, and hardware;
- contracts, renewals, migrations, and active projects;
- known customer pain;
- Worldpay relationship history.

### Opportunity

- account-specific payments-plus-technology storyline;
- hypothesized customer value;
- likely problems or opportunities to test through discovery;
- target Genius product or capability, if one is already credible;
- value for the customer and Worldpay;
- competitive position;
- timing and trigger;
- proof, reference, or product answer required;
- disqualifiers.

### Relationship map

- known Worldpay contacts;
- relationship strength;
- missing buying-center relationships;
- executive sponsor;
- decision maker;
- technical evaluator;
- operational influencer;
- financial buyer;
- marketing stakeholder;
- procurement/legal path;
- warm-introduction owner.

### Action plan

- next customer commitment;
- next Worldpay/Genius action;
- owner and due date;
- upcoming QBR/SBR or event;
- risks and blockers;
- status: advance, research, nurture, defer, or close.

## Widening the Customer Relationship

Worldpay's strongest contacts may sit in finance or payments. Genius opportunities often require a wider relationship map.

| Buying center | Typical ownership | Relevant Genius conversations | Relationship-building path |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Finance / payments** | CFO, treasury, payments, finance operations | Payment economics, gateways, reporting, fraud, total cost | Start with the Worldpay relationship; ask who owns related technology and operating decisions |
| **Technology** | CIO, CTO, restaurant systems, architecture | POS, integrations, security, hardware, implementation, roadmap | Request a technology-roadmap introduction or QBR/SBR participation |
| **Operations** | COO, restaurant operations, field/franchise operations | Back office, labor, inventory, throughput, support, rollout | Use operational pain and store-level outcomes to earn the introduction |
| **Marketing / digital** | CMO, digital, loyalty, customer experience | DMB, loyalty, guest data, promotions, digital ordering | Connect to campaign speed, consistency, engagement, and guest experience |
| **Franchise leadership** | Franchisee councils and large operators | Deployment, economics, support, adoption, hardware, training | Work through corporate and the RM; distinguish corporate from franchisee value |
| **Procurement / legal** | Sourcing, procurement, legal | Commercial structure, contracts, compliance | Engage after value and sponsorship are established |

Every active Blue Sheet should identify:

- one Worldpay relationship;
- one technology relationship;
- one operations or marketing relationship, depending on the product;
- one executive sponsor or path to sponsorship.

If those relationships do not exist, relationship creation becomes the next action.

### Relationship-building sequence

The normal progression is:

1. Worldpay RM sponsors the initial introduction.
2. Todd establishes credibility and learns the customer's priorities.
3. Todd and the RM identify the next buying-center relationship to add.
4. The team develops a relevant payments-plus-technology point of view.
5. Todd earns participation in QBRs, SBRs, roadmap reviews, and working sessions.
6. A specific discovery or product motion begins when fit, timing, and customer interest align.

This sequence can start well before the customer declares pain or begins an RFP.

## Product-Targeting Rule

| Current status | Recommended motion |
|---|---|
| POS migration active and decision still open | POS + payments discovery may be appropriate |
| POS selected or deployed within 24 months | Do not lead with POS; look at DMB, hardware, drive-thru, payments, loyalty, or implementation gaps |
| POS established but contract timing unclear | Research renewal, satisfaction, and gaps before positioning a product |
| Existing Genius customer | Protect adoption and pursue verified expansion; do not treat as a new logo |
| Deployment struggling | Offer problem-solving and adjacent support; do not assume replacement |
| Stack layer unknown | Treat it as a research gap, not automatic whitespace |

## QBR / SBR Strategy

Before the meeting:

- agree with the RM on the customer issue and desired outcome;
- identify participants and missing buying centers;
- prepare a concise account/stack view;
- choose two or three discovery questions;
- avoid an unsolicited product presentation.

During the meeting:

- listen for business and operating priorities;
- ask questions in the customer's language;
- connect finance pain to technology and operational implications;
- offer a follow-up working session only where there is a credible fit.

After the meeting:

- update the Blue Sheet;
- record new relationships and evidence;
- assign one next action, owner, and date;
- close the loop with the RM.
