## Executive read

Pollo Campero is a Guatemala-born, family-controlled international chicken brand owned within CMI Foods. Its global center is Guatemala City; Campero USA operates from Dallas. The U.S. business is in a rapid expansion cycle: Pollo Campero stated in June 2026 that it had grown beyond 150 U.S. restaurants and was targeting 250 by 2030. The growth rate makes technology standardization, rollout capacity, support quality, payments architecture, digital-order integration, gift continuity, reporting, and franchise/corporate governance strategically important.

The Global Payments/Genius opportunity has two separate streams:

1. **Worldpay referral:** currently stalled because the merchant-side path is not responding. This needs an owner and disposition, not another uncoordinated chase.
2. **Potential RFI:** the NDA must be executed, the RFI reviewed, and the correct response team assigned. The scope and deadline are not yet canonical, so this should not be forecast as a qualified RFI until the document is received and triaged.

Internal field intelligence describes a split Aloha/Oracle Simphony estate, an active migration toward Simphony, Fiserv in-store processing, Olo Pay/Stripe online payments, Valutec gift cards, and more than $200 million in annual card volume. Those facts make this both a restaurant-platform and payments opportunity—but each remains field-sourced until Campero or the RFI confirms it.

The central strategic question is whether Genius can still influence the U.S. standard before Oracle migration lock-in. If the core POS decision is already contractually committed, the posture must shift from broad displacement to a specific modular, payment, hardware, deployment, or integration wedge.

## Competitive dynamics

### Oracle Simphony

Primary threat. Oracle can argue global scale, enterprise security, broad integrations, centralized channel/menu management, KDS/kiosk breadth, analytics, offline resilience, and integrated payments. If Campero has already contracted and deployed deeply, displacement probability falls sharply.

Questions:

- Is the Oracle decision final or still challengeable?
- What has actually been contracted?
- How many sites are live, piloting, scheduled, or untouched?
- What business problem drove the selection?
- Where has Oracle underperformed, if anywhere?
- Are payments included or separately contestable?

### NCR/Aloha

Legacy incumbent in part of the estate. Determine version, support state, hardware lifecycle, customization, integrations, and why Campero is leaving or retaining it.

### Fiserv

In-store processor according to field intelligence. Determine acquiring, gateway, device, token, settlement, reporting, contract, and pricing ownership. A payments displacement case needs quantified merchant value and an executable transition plan.

### Olo Pay / Stripe

Digital incumbent with stated fraud/chargeback value. Determine whether Olo Pay is mandated, preferred, or contestable; token portability; chargeback performance; approval rates; reporting; commercial structure; and how in-store/digital fragmentation affects finance and operations.

### Valutec

Gift-card continuity is a critical dependency. Determine liability, outstanding balances, franchise settlement, omnichannel redemption, processor integration, reporting, promotions, and migration requirements.

### Loyalty/rewards

The current U.S. program is app-based, tiered, supports in-store/online redemption, and migrated users from a prior program. Historical Punchh knowledge is useful context, but the current vendor and architecture must be verified.

## RFI discovery and diligence checklist

### Scope and decision

- What exact products and countries are in scope?
- Is this an information request, competitive replacement, benchmark, or procurement requirement?
- Is there an incumbent preference or preselected direction?
- What are the mandatory requirements and weighted criteria?
- Who signs, recommends, validates, and can veto?
- What is the intended contract and rollout timeline?

### Estate

- Exact store count and corporate/franchise mix.
- POS distribution by product/version/location.
- Oracle contracted/deployed/pipeline counts.
- Hardware inventory, age, peripherals, network, and replacement plan.
- Drive-thru, KDS, kiosk, DMB, back-office, labor, inventory, and reporting systems.
- International-market requirements and exceptions.

### Payments

- Card volume by in-store, digital, market, tender, card-present/not-present, and franchise entity.
- Processor, acquirer, gateway, devices, encryption/tokenization, settlement, reporting, fraud, disputes, and contract terms.
- Olo Pay/Stripe economics and performance.
- Debit, contactless, alternative tenders, gift, and cross-border needs.
- Data ownership and portability.

### Digital and guest

- Olo scope and contract.
- App/web ownership.
- Loyalty provider and migration status.
- Menu, pricing, promotions, offers, and availability architecture.
- Third-party delivery and order aggregation.
- Guest data, consent, personalization, and reporting ownership.

### Operations and deployment

- Required implementation rate and blackout periods.
- Pilot structure and success criteria.
- Training, field services, help desk, SLAs, spares, swap, monitoring, escalation, and disaster recovery.
- Franchisee approval, economics, adoption, and support responsibilities.
- Spanish-language and international support requirements.

### Security and legal

- PCI scope, encryption, tokenization, data residency, privacy, cyber insurance, audit rights, accessibility, and incident response.
- NDA restrictions, permitted references, subcontractors, and data-room controls.
- Contract assignment, termination, transition services, price protection, and change control.

## Bid/no-bid gates

Bid only if the team can answer yes to most of the following:

- Is the decision genuinely open?
- Is there meaningful scope not already locked to Oracle?
- Does Genius have proven product fit for the mandatory requirements?
- Can Global Payments meet the payment, debit, fraud, device, and market requirements?
- Can the team support the required countries and rollout schedule?
- Is there a credible executive/business sponsor or fair evaluation path?
- Can the response team produce proof—not roadmap promises—for critical requirements?
- Are implementation/support economics viable?
- Can Olo, gift, loyalty, and data continuity be handled without unacceptable risk?

Red flags:

- RFI is only procurement cover for an Oracle decision already made.
- Requirements demand unsupported countries/features on an unrealistic timetable.
- No access to clarification or discovery.
- Oracle migration is substantially complete.
- Payments are contractually unavailable and the remaining product scope is uneconomic.
- Critical claims depend on uncommitted roadmap.
