## Executive Summary
Red Robin Gourmet Burgers, Inc. (NASDAQ: RRGB) is a publicly traded casual-dining brand founded in 1969 and headquartered in Englewood, Colorado. At fiscal year-end December 28, 2025, it operated 475 restaurants—385 company-owned and 90 franchised—across the United States and one Canadian province. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $1.210 billion, down 3.1%, and comparable restaurant revenue declined 0.3% excluding deferred-loyalty effects, but restaurant-level operating margin improved to 12.7% from 10.8%. In Q2 2026, comparable restaurant revenue increased 1.3%, traffic declined only 0.2%, average check rose 1.5%, and restaurant-level operating margin reached 14.7%, the strongest second-quarter result since 2022. Total revenue and adjusted EBITDA nevertheless remained below the prior-year quarter, so the business should be characterized as improving but still constrained.

President and CEO Dave Pace is executing the **First Choice Plan**, which focuses on guest value and traffic, restaurant execution, labor efficiency, Managing Partner empowerment, balance-sheet repair, and reinvestment in restaurants and technology. The most important current event is refranchising: on September 1, 2026, Red Robin completed the sale of 108 company-owned restaurants for approximately $89.4 million and expects eight more units to close by fiscal year-end for $6.6 million. The full 116-unit program is expected to generate approximately $96 million, reduce debt, support refinancing, and improve financial flexibility. It also changes the account's buying structure by adding three major operators—Op Burgers, Kuber, and Evergreen Dining—and increasing the importance of franchise standards, operator economics, implementation responsibility, and commercial consistency.

The leadership team is unusually relevant to account strategy. Dave Pace leads the turnaround; Mark Graff became CFO in May 2026; Jesse Griffith leads operations and franchise relations; John McLaughlin, Chief Transformation Officer, leads refranchising, supply chain, and facilities; and Deena DePhilips, SVP and Chief Technology Officer, owns digital platforms, restaurant technology, cybersecurity, data and analytics, enterprise systems, infrastructure, operations, and AI strategy. Scott Hudler begins his transition into the Chief Marketing Officer role on September 8, with responsibility for brand, digital marketing, loyalty, culinary innovation, guest insights, and communications. Because both McLaughlin and DePhilips can be abbreviated “CTO,” their full titles should always be used.

The known technology environment is a mature, integrated stack with material qualification gaps. RB confirms Worldpay as the payments relationship at high confidence and identifies Avery Churchwell as the Worldpay RM. The Worldpay Master Account Plan lists NCR Aloha POS, Ziosk pay-at-table, Olo digital ordering, and MonkeyMedia catering. Ziosk's public site currently displays Red Robin as a customer, and Red Robin's own site confirms app-based ordering, rewards, curbside, pickup, and delivery. The 2025 Form 10-K says Red Robin completed infrastructure modernization and kitchen-display-system upgrades, spent $13.7 million on technology and infrastructure capital, and still has some aging systems despite replacing substantial hardware in recent years. Exact vendors, versions, store cohorts, Worldpay scope, contract terms, and franchise coverage remain to be confirmed.

Avery's September 4 account update is the central internal signal. She said recent reading suggested Red Robin may be in a better operating position and should be reconsidered as a prospecting possibility. The current internal hypothesis is a **pay-at-table replacement plus payment-savings** motion, but Avery did not report a customer-confirmed buying event. The Worldpay plan identifies possible Ziosk dissatisfaction; the specific customer language, affected locations, operational impact, decision owner, and sourcing status are not yet established. Public results give partial support to Avery's assessment, while Red Robin's debt, declining total revenue, and capital discipline argue for a narrow, measurable, low-disruption approach.

The recommended posture is discovery-first. Begin with Avery to validate her evidence, contacts, Worldpay scope, renewal dates, and the source of the Ziosk concern. Then map Deena DePhilips, Jesse Griffith, Mark Graff, Scott Hudler, corporate procurement, franchise leadership, and the three new operators. Build a store-cohort view of NCR versions, Ziosk coverage, payment devices and processing, Olo modules, KDS upgrades, and corporate-versus-franchise ownership. Only then develop a pilot-level value case around pay-at-table, payment economics, labor efficiency, table turns, guest convenience, uptime, and support simplicity. Red Robin should remain a P3 prospect until a sponsor, dated contract window, active buying event, or verified operational problem emerges.

Immediate actions: (1) schedule a 30-minute Red Robin review with Avery; (2) capture the exact Worldpay footprint and renewal dates; (3) validate the reported Ziosk dissatisfaction; (4) identify a warm path to Deena DePhilips or the relevant restaurant-technology owner; (5) map decision authority across corporate and newly refranchised restaurants; and (6) define a narrow discovery agenda and success metrics before presenting Genius.

## Key Discovery Questions

### Business and Operating Priorities
- What are current leadership's top strategic priorities?
- What operating metrics (comps, traffic, margin) are under the most pressure right now?
- Is there an active turnaround, growth push, or ownership/leadership change underway?
- What is driving any current technology or vendor evaluation, if one exists?

### Loyalty and Digital
- What loyalty platform is currently in use, if any?
- What percentage of transactions come through loyalty or digital channels?
- What are the current app, online-ordering, and delivery platforms?
- Who owns digital engagement and customer data internally?

### Payments
- Who processes in-store and digital payments today?
- Is there a single payments gateway/acquirer, or a fragmented setup across channels?
- When do the current payments agreements renew?
- Are there known pain points (fees, chargebacks, fraud, reliability)?

### Restaurant Technology
- What POS system is currently deployed?
- What hardware is installed, and is there a known refresh or support deadline?
- What systems handle KDS, back office, inventory, and labor management?
- Are there known integration or data-quality issues across systems?

### Decision Process
- Who owns technology strategy and vendor decisions?
- Who owns loyalty, payments, and digital commerce internally?
- Is there an existing Global Payments/Genius relationship or contact?
- What are the next known renewal, budgeting, or vendor-review dates?
