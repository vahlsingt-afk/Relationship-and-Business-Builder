# Slim Chickens Background Brief

Prepared by: Todd Vahlsing

Purpose: Account background and discovery preparation

Current posture: Pre-engagement research and qualification — channel and stakeholder coordination required

## Executive Summary

Slim Chickens is a founder-led, franchised fast-casual chicken brand established in Fayetteville, Arkansas, in 2003. Co-founders Tom Gordon and Greg Smart remain active as chief executive officer and chief brand officer. 10 Point Capital has been a minority investment partner since 2019, supporting aggressive domestic and international franchise growth.

The account has strategic relevance because Worldpay is a confirmed internal relationship and Slim Chickens is publicly identified as a PAR customer. Olo supports digital ordering, while the brand’s own app and rewards program create additional digital and data dependencies. Internal intelligence also identifies an existing technology-team relationship and overlapping franchise ownership with Freddy’s.

The opportunity is not a broad, uncoordinated platform pursuit. PAR and RDS channel considerations must be clarified first, and the current CIO relationship requires careful stakeholder navigation. The most credible path is to use the existing technology-team access to identify the day-to-day leader, protect the Worldpay position, and pursue a specific gap in payments, hardware, digital menu boards, back office, franchise deployment, or international consistency.

## Current Business Situation

Slim Chickens has pursued rapid unit development since 10 Point Capital’s investment. The investor’s retained profile describes a large development pipeline, while franchise materials emphasize multi-unit growth in the United States, United Kingdom, and additional international markets. The brand’s growth model depends on experienced operators, strong unit economics, disciplined openings, and a repeatable support system.

Technology supports a multi-channel operating model that includes drive-thru, dine-in, first-party digital ordering, delivery, app-based rewards, and international franchise operations. PAR identifies Slim Chickens as a customer, and the current ecosystem record confirms Olo for online ordering and Worldpay for payments. However, precise product scope, regional variation, and RDS ownership remain unresolved.

Internal intelligence elevates stakeholder management as the immediate issue. There is an existing relationship with the technology team, but the day-to-day technology leader still needs to be identified and the CIO relationship handled carefully. Freddy’s and Slim Chickens also share franchise ownership in parts of their networks, creating a useful access and intelligence bridge if the two buying paths remain distinct.

## Opportunity Assessment

*These are hypotheses, not confirmed opportunities.*

### Protect and expand Worldpay selectively

Clarify merchant scope, digital and card-present roles, economics, renewal dates, and franchise participation. Use existing payments access as the intelligence channel before proposing additional products.

### Channel-coordinated restaurant technology adjacencies

Potential whitespace may exist in digital menu boards, hardware, back office, device management, labor, analytics, or international deployment. Each hypothesis must be validated with the technology team and coordinated through the RDS model.

### Shared-franchise network leverage

Overlapping Freddy’s and Slim Chickens operators may provide insight into support quality, purchasing authority, and unmet needs. Treat the overlap as an access network, not evidence of a common decision.

### International operating consistency

Rapid international development may create differences in payments, ordering, support, reporting, and technology standards. A repeatable multi-market solution could be relevant where the brand or master franchisee controls the decision.

## Key Discovery Questions

### Stakeholders and Channel

- Who is the day-to-day technology leader, and what is the CIO’s role in vendor decisions?
- Who owns the RDS relationship and what referral, joint-sale, or dealer-led model applies?
- Who is the Worldpay relationship manager, and which introductions are appropriate?
- Which Freddy’s/Slim Chickens franchise groups can provide useful intelligence or access?

### Technology and Operations

- Which PAR products are deployed, at how many locations, and under what commercial model?
- What systems support loyalty, back office, inventory, labor, KDS, hardware, and digital menu boards?
- Where do franchisees experience integration, support, reporting, or rollout friction?
- How consistent is the stack across corporate, franchise, and international restaurants?

### Payments and Digital

- What are Worldpay’s card-present, digital, gateway, and acquiring roles?
- How does Olo connect to the POS, rewards program, payments, and guest data?
- Who owns customer data and digital engagement, and what performance gaps exist?
- When do payments, POS, ordering, or loyalty agreements renew?

### Growth and Economics

- What is the current restaurant count, opening pipeline, and development cadence?
- Which markets or formats are most important over the next 24 months?
- What unit-level economics or operational constraints could slow development?
- How are technology costs and deployment responsibilities allocated to franchisees?

## Risks and Cautions

- PAR is an incumbent, and RDS may control or materially influence the customer relationship.
- The CIO relationship requires deliberate coordination; poorly sequenced outreach could close access rather than expand it.
- Public growth counts and executive titles vary by source date and require current confirmation.
- International and franchise variation may produce different buyers, vendors, and economics.
- Shared franchise ownership with Freddy’s is useful context but does not create shared authority.
- Do not infer a broad PAR product deployment or a broad Worldpay footprint from logo-level or relationship evidence alone.

## Recommended Engagement Strategy

Start inside the existing relationship network. Align with the Worldpay relationship manager, the RDS owner, and the internal contact who knows the Slim Chickens technology team. Identify the day-to-day technology leader before approaching the CIO, and agree on the purpose and sequence of engagement.

Use discovery to isolate one customer problem with measurable franchisee or operating impact. The strongest narrative is likely to connect operational simplicity and growth readiness with payments economics. Maintain a separate account strategy from Freddy’s while using shared franchise operators to improve intelligence and access.

## Immediate Actions

- Confirm the current Worldpay relationship manager and RDS/dealer owner.
- Identify the day-to-day technology leader and document the CIO engagement plan.
- Verify the deployed PAR products, Olo scope, Worldpay footprint, and contract dates.
- Map corporate, franchise, and international technology decision rights.
- Identify overlapping Freddy’s franchise groups and the best intelligence or introduction path.
- Select one dated customer trigger and one narrow adjacency hypothesis before outreach.

## Bottom Line

Slim Chickens is a worthwhile background-brief account because it combines sustained franchise growth, a confirmed Worldpay relationship, an existing technology-team access path, and potential whitespace around a partially verified technology estate. It should not be approached as an open-field POS pursuit. Success depends on careful stakeholder navigation, explicit RDS coordination, and a narrow problem-led motion that improves franchisee economics or execution without ignoring the installed PAR and Olo environment.
