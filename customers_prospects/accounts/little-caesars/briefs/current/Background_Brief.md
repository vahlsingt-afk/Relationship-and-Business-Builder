## Executive Summary
Little Caesars is a privately held, family-owned global pizza brand founded in Detroit in 1959 and owned within the Ilitch Companies portfolio. The brand describes itself as the world's third-largest pizza chain, with thousands of restaurants in 30 countries and territories and coverage in all 50 U.S. states. The 2026 U.S. franchise disclosure data reported 4,374 domestic outlets at year-end 2025—3,788 franchised and 586 company-owned. Because the company is private, consolidated revenue and systemwide sales are not publicly disclosed and should not be estimated.

The strategic posture is growth-oriented and operationally disciplined: global expansion, franchisee profitability, value leadership, convenience, digital innovation, and simple execution. International growth continued with entry into the United Arab Emirates in 2025 and Malaysia in 2026. The leadership structure changed January 1, 2026: longtime CEO Dave Scrivano became vice chairman; Paula Vissing became President and CEO, Global Retail; Ed Gleich became President and CEO, Global Services; and Leigh Burnside became CFO and Chief Administrative Officer. Anita Klopfenstein is Global Chief Information and Digital Officer, supported by Keith Faigin as CIO, Restaurant Systems; Lance Shinabarger as CIO, Shared Technologies and Applications; and Afia Phillips as CISO.

Little Caesars has a sophisticated proprietary restaurant and digital estate. CaesarVision / CV Cloud is the core restaurant platform. World Wide Technology describes it as a cloud-native POS and kitchen system with forecasting, inventory and quality controls, order slotting, analytics, delivery integration, and international scalability. Deliverect integrates global third-party delivery into the POS, centralizes menu and price management, and supports Pizza Portal pickup workflows. Little Caesars also operates its own mobile/web ordering, Pizza Portal heated pickup stations, and, as of April 2026, a ChatGPT ordering experience that uses the existing app and deep links for checkout. Worldpay is the confirmed payments relationship; Verifone is identified in the device estate; and Mashgin appears in a limited self-service concept test.

The strongest opportunity signal is customer-generated international interest. During a 2026 client experience center visit for a Canadian processing discussion, Little Caesars saw the Genius installation and asked about international capabilities spanning register/POS, kiosks, back office, and adjacent systems. Avery Churchwell, the Worldpay RM, described Little Caesars as his largest brand at roughly $6 million in annual Worldpay revenue and as receptive to adjacent products, while cautioning that the account often takes about two years to act. The opportunity is strategic cultivation—not a qualified sales cycle—and the homegrown platform makes a broad domestic POS-replacement pitch unlikely.

Internal discussions point to potential international fragmentation and cost pressure. India and Thailand were cited as markets where inexpensive local systems make enterprise economics difficult. Little Caesars reportedly abandoned an earlier Oracle one-stop discussion because it was too expensive, and a separate consultant discussion described FreedomPay kiosk economics in Spain as problematic. These points are useful discovery context but remain unverified. The more credible posture is modular and country-specific: determine where CaesarVision is live, where local systems remain, and whether Genius can fill a defined register, kiosk, back-office, payments, DMB, integration, or data gap.

The immediate account issue is relationship recovery. On September 4, Avery reported three open items and no response to repeated emails for approximately a week and a half, despite a technical-support call earlier that week. Before expanding the motion, consolidate those requests into one dated owner/action list and use the existing support interaction to re-establish responsiveness. Then identify the contact who raised the international question, refresh the Genius capability and country-coverage asset, and earn a consultative discovery session with Avery visibly leading the relationship.

Recommended strategy: protect the proprietary core, qualify country-by-country whitespace, and avoid a generic demo or premium transformation pitch. Map Worldpay scope, CaesarVision/CV Cloud coverage, local systems, payments, devices, kiosks, DMB, delivery, compliance, and support requirements across named markets. Advance only when Little Caesars defines the problem, target countries, business owner, success criteria, budget, and timing.

Immediate actions: (1) meet with Avery and resolve the three stalled items; (2) confirm the client-center contact and exact international request; (3) refresh the capability and geographic-coverage asset; (4) build a country technology map beginning with Canada, Spain, India, and Thailand; and (5) develop a modular pilot only after the customer validates a problem and decision path.

## Key Discovery Questions

### Business and Operating Priorities
- What are current leadership's top strategic priorities?
- What operating metrics (comps, traffic, margin) are under the most pressure right now?
- Is there an active turnaround, growth push, or ownership/leadership change underway?
- What is driving any current technology or vendor evaluation, if one exists?

### Loyalty and Digital
- What loyalty platform is currently in use, if any?
- What percentage of transactions come through loyalty or digital channels?
- What are the current app, online-ordering, and delivery platforms?
- Who owns digital engagement and customer data internally?

### Payments
- Who processes in-store and digital payments today?
- Is there a single payments gateway/acquirer, or a fragmented setup across channels?
- When do the current payments agreements renew?
- Are there known pain points (fees, chargebacks, fraud, reliability)?

### Restaurant Technology
- What POS system is currently deployed?
- What hardware is installed, and is there a known refresh or support deadline?
- What systems handle KDS, back office, inventory, and labor management?
- Are there known integration or data-quality issues across systems?

### Decision Process
- Who owns technology strategy and vendor decisions?
- Who owns loyalty, payments, and digital commerce internally?
- Is there an existing Global Payments/Genius relationship or contact?
- What are the next known renewal, budgeting, or vendor-review dates?
